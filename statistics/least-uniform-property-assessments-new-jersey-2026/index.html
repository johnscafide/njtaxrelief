Where property assessments are least uniform in New Jersey

A town can have a perfectly ordinary tax rate and still assess similar homes unevenly. This ranking uses the residential coefficient of deviation published by the New Jersey Division of Taxation to show where assessment-to-sale ratios vary most within the same municipality.

This is an assessment-consistency ranking, not an appeal prediction. A higher coefficient means residential assessments vary more around the municipality's own norm. It does not prove that a specific property is over-assessed, does not replace market-value evidence, and does not change the governed Watchdog Score or ROBUST model.

How to read coefficient of deviation

It measures spread, not tax level

The coefficient of deviation measures how dispersed individual assessment-to-sale ratios are around a municipality's typical ratio. It answers whether assessments are being applied consistently, not whether the town's overall ratio is high or low.

Higher means less uniform

A larger coefficient means more variation among residential assessments relative to market evidence. Watchdog's separate uniformity score converts that evidence into a 0-100 consistency measure, where higher is better.

One number is not a case

Municipal unevenness can make closer review worthwhile, but an individual appeal still needs property-specific evidence. Watchdog does not turn a town-level coefficient into a property-level win probability.
