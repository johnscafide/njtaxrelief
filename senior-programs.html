Senior property tax relief: Stay NJ and the Senior Freeze

If you're 65 or older and own your NJ home, these two programs do the heavy lifting. Stay NJ cuts your bill in half, up to $6,500 a year. The Senior Freeze reimburses your tax increases. One PAS-1 form applies you to both, plus ANCHOR.

Calculate my Stay NJ credit File the PAS-1
One form covers all three programs. Seniors 65+ apply for ANCHOR, Stay NJ, and the Senior Freeze with the PAS-1 combined application. Deadline: November 2, 2026. File at propertytaxrelief.nj.gov or get step-by-step help here.
Stay NJ

Stay NJ: half off your property tax bill

Signed into law in 2023, paying out quarterly since February 2026. The FY2027 budget kept the program but tightened who qualifies.

Program change, now law Updated June 30, 2026

The FY2027 budget changed who qualifies

The $6,500 maximum stays. But the income limit dropped from $500,000 to about $200,000, and the credit is now capped by income:

Income $100,000 or lessup to $6,500
$100,000 to $150,000up to $5,000
$150,000 to $200,000up to $4,000
Over $200,000not eligible
Eligibility uses NJ gross income, not federal AGI. Exact thresholds will be confirmed by the NJ Division of Taxation. The Senior Freeze was not cut.

Senior Freeze (PTR)

Senior Freeze: your tax increases, reimbursed

The Senior Freeze pays you back every dollar your property taxes rise above your "base year" amount. The older your base year, the bigger your check.

How it actually works: you still receive and pay your full tax bill every year. Then the State reimburses you for the amount above your base year. If your 2026 taxes are $6,200 and your base year was $4,100, you get a $2,100 check.

Senior Freeze requirements

  • Age 65 or older, or permanently disabledDisabled means receiving Social Security or Railroad Retirement disability benefits
  • NJ resident for 10+ consecutive yearsTen straight years living in New Jersey
  • In your current home for 3+ yearsCounted from when you moved in
  • Income limit: $168,268 (2024), $172,475 (2025)The limit rises slightly each year
  • Property taxes paid in fullFor the year you're claiming
First-time filers: your first eligible year sets your base year, and only increases after it get reimbursed. Applying sooner locks in a lower base year and bigger future checks.

Common questions

Senior program FAQ

Does the Senior Freeze actually stop my taxes from going up?
No. Your actual tax bill still goes up each year. The Senior Freeze reimburses you for the increase above your base year amount, so your bill rises but the State sends you a check for the difference. Your net out-of-pocket stays close to your base year level.
Did the 2026 state budget change Stay NJ?
Yes. The FY2027 budget, passed in June 2026, kept the $6,500 maximum credit but lowered the income limit from $500,000 to about $200,000 and tiered the benefit: up to $6,500 for income under $100,000, $5,000 from $100,000 to $150,000, and $4,000 from $150,000 to $200,000. Above about $200,000 in NJ gross income you no longer qualify. NJ gross income is not the same as your federal AGI. The Senior Freeze was not cut. Exact thresholds will be confirmed by the NJ Division of Taxation.
When did Stay NJ start paying out?
Payments began in February 2026 for 2024 benefit year applications. The program became law in 2023 but took time to implement. Payments go out quarterly, in February, May, August, and November, by direct deposit or check.
My spouse passed away. Do I still qualify?
You may. Surviving spouses qualify for Stay NJ from age 55 if they meet the other requirements. For the Senior Freeze and ANCHOR, call the hotline at 1-888-238-1233 to confirm how benefits transfer in your situation.
I just turned 65 this year. When can I first apply?
You can file the PAS-1 for the benefit year you turned 65. Do it as soon as you're eligible: that first year sets your Senior Freeze base year, and a lower base year means bigger reimbursements every year after.
Is Stay NJ taxable income?
The NJ Division of Taxation has not classified Stay NJ credits as taxable income for NJ state tax purposes. Federal treatment of state benefits can be more complicated, so ask a tax professional about your federal filing.
What if I missed the PAS-1 deadline?
Call 1-888-238-1233 right away and do not assume the benefit is gone. The Division of Taxation sometimes accepts late applications with a written explanation. For future years, put November 2 on your calendar now.
Can I pay someone to fill the PAS-1 out?
Yes. If you pay a preparer to complete the PAS-1, they are required to sign the form as the paid preparer.
Do I only fill the form out once?
No, the PAS-1 is filed every year. A practical tip: your tax professional can complete it for you right after doing your income taxes, while all the numbers are in front of them.
