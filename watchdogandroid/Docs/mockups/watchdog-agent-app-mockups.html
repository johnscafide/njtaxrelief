Watchdog Agent App for iPhone and Android

Ten screens, drawn natively for each platform, for New Jersey agents who work their clients and farm from the car, the curb and the kitchen table at an open house. Every screen runs on data Watchdog already has: the Monday digest, the Watchdog Score, the tax checkup and the buyer true cost card.

Platforms
iOS, following the Human Interface Guidelines with Liquid Glass bars. Android, following Material 3.
Frames
iPhone 393 × 852 pt and Pixel 412 × 892 dp, shown at 1:1 on wide screens. Long screens scroll inside the frame.
Brand
Navy, gold and teal on paper, 24 px flat cards, Plus Jakarta Sans. The Watchdog Score, powered by the ROBUST Framework.
Data
Sample New Jersey homes and a fictional agent. No owner names or owner mailing addresses anywhere.

01 · First run

Welcome and sign in

An agent decides in the first minute whether an app is for them. This screen names the audience, says what Watchdog watches, and lists the three setup steps that make every other screen useful: add past clients, draw a farm, turn on the Monday email. Sign-in uses email or a passkey on the same account as the Agent Desk, so nothing is set up twice.

iOS
Navy header runs under the status bar. Capsule buttons; the passkey opens the system sign-in sheet.
Android
Edge to edge. Filled and outlined Material buttons; Credential Manager handles the passkey.

02 · Home tab

Today

Today answers one question before the agent has parked the car: who should I call this week, and why? It is the Monday email as a living screen. The count of changes comes first, then the few tasks that need a tap (send 12 checkups, call about a bill that rose $612), then each change with the home, the relationship and the public-record reason. Search and scan sit at the top because looking up a house is what agents do most between appointments.

iOS
Large title with toolbar buttons in glass. Floating tab bar with Search as its own button.
Android
Search bar at the top, extended FAB for Scan, navigation bar with four destinations.

03 · Property detail

Property

This is the screen an agent pulls up on a doorstep or across a kitchen table. The Watchdog Score and its verdict come first, then what homeowners actually ask: this year's bill, next year's at the new rate, and whether the assessment holds up at today's prices. The value line shows the price the assessment needs against what similar homes sold for. Nearby sales, home facts and the ROBUST breakdown sit below, and the three actions that turn research into outreach stay pinned.

iOS
Glass back and share buttons; the action bar floats above the home indicator and the tab bar hides on push.
Android
Top app bar with back arrow; bottom action area with filled and tonal buttons; predictive back returns to the list.

04 · Listing scan

Scan a listing

Agents meet listings on the street and in text threads more than in a desktop browser. Scan puts the browser extension's panel on the phone. Point the camera at a For Sale sign (the app reads the sign's QR rider and confirms the parcel by location) or paste a Zillow, Realtor.com or Redfin link. The price check tells a buyer's agent in seconds whether the tax is in line for the price or likely to move after a revaluation.

iOS · camera mode
The result opens in a medium-detent sheet so the sign stays in view.
Android · paste mode
Links shared from a listing app land here through the Android share target. Both modes exist on both platforms.

05 · Clients tab

Clients

Past clients are an agent's best source of repeat business, but only when there is a real reason to reach out. Each row leads with the home, then the relationship and the agent's own CRM reference, never a public-record owner name. A status chip says what changed and the next action says what to do about it. After final bills go out, the checkup banner gathers every ready checkup into one send.

iOS
Segmented control for the three lists; swipe a row to send or snooze.
Android
Filter chips for the lists; extended FAB adds clients from contacts or a CSV.

06 · Farm tab

Farm

A farm is the neighborhood an agent wants to be known in. Coloring every parcel by Watchdog Score shows at a glance where assessments are strong or stretched, and gold dots mark homes that sold in the last year. The sheet sums up turnover from public deed records at the neighborhood level: sales, prices and permits. Watchdog never tags a single home as a likely seller.

iOS
Map under glass controls, as in Apple Maps; the sheet has medium and large detents.
Android
Floating search bar and chips over the map, a standard bottom sheet, and a FAB to draw a new area.

07 · Marketing tab

Marketing and sharing

Marketing turns the data into something an agent sends under their own name. Postcards and email updates show status and results at a glance. The buyer true cost card is the buyer-side hook: one tap shares the real monthly cost of a listing, including the property tax, with the agent's contact card on it, through the phone's own share sheet.

iOS
System share sheet with a custom preview of the card.
Android
Android Sharesheet with a rich preview and a Copy action.

08 · Brief and Voice

Watchdog Intelligence

Watchdog Intelligence reads the week's changes and writes the short version an agent wants from an assistant: three things, with numbers and sources. Watchdog Intelligence Voice takes follow-up questions while the agent drives between showings. The rotating spectrum border sits only on the outer briefing card, and only the word Intelligence gets the spectrum color.

iOS
Pushed full screen from Today; the composer floats in glass above the home indicator.
Android
The top app bar keeps the full product name; the mic is a FAB-sized control.

09 · Notifications

Alerts

Notifications are why the app belongs on a phone. Each one arrives when something changed for a specific home and says what changed in the notification itself, so the agent can decide without opening the app. The Monday brief lands at 8:00 AM, the same time as the email.

iOS
Grouped on the Lock Screen; a long press shows Send checkup and Open.
Android
A notification group with action buttons; channels match the alert switches in Settings.

10 · Settings

Settings

Settings mirror the Agent Desk. The Monday email is on by default and turns off with one switch. Each alert type has its own switch, so an agent can keep deed alerts and silence town news, and quiet hours keep Watchdog from buzzing during dinner.

iOS
Inset grouped list with teal switches.
Android
Material list with section headers; switches show a check when on.

Dark mode

Today, after dark

Agents check their phones at 6 AM and after evening showings. Dark mode keeps the board look (navy score card, tinted cards for tax, value and sales) with colors tuned for a dark ground rather than inverted. The Watchdog Intelligence card stays a white surface, as the brand rule requires. Use App theme at the top to see every screen dark.

Both
Follows the system setting, with an override in Settings.

Dark mode

Property, after dark

The score dial, tax and value cards keep their meaning in dark. Gold and teal are lifted so the arc, bars and value line stay readable on navy, and the status tints drop to deep tones so white text stays on top.

Design system

One brand, two native grammars

Brand colors, type and cards are shared across both apps. Navigation, sheets, buttons, switches and lists follow each platform, so the app feels at home on either phone.

  1. Put a clickable prototype in five agents' handsRun Today, Property and Scan as a tap-through on their own phones during a real week, and watch where they reach for search versus the camera.
  2. Share targets on both platformsA listing link shared from any app should open straight into the scan result. This is the fastest route to daily use.
  3. Widgets and Lock ScreenA "This week" widget with the change count and the top change, plus a checkup-season Live Activity in February.
