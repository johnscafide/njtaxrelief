## 1. What Watchdog is (say it this way)

Watchdog is New Jersey property intelligence. It pulls the public property record, assessment and tax history, Chapter 123 context, verified sales (SR1A), permits, municipal context, and flood or environmental screening into one property view, with the source and date attached to every number.

For agents, Watchdog is the evidence companion beside the MLS. The MLS tells you what is for sale. Watchdog tells you what the property and the town look like underneath the listing, and what changed since you last looked.

Master brand: **Watchdog**. Domain: **www.watchdogindex.com**. The platform is not "Watchdog Index" (that is a product family for place measurement). Never write "ROBUST Score." The correct phrase is "the Watchdog Score, powered by the ROBUST Framework." The intelligence layer is always the exact words **Watchdog Intelligence** (never "Intel").

Founder: John Scafide. TikTok: @thetaxwatchdog (existing audience is NJ homeowners learning about property taxes and relief programs). LinkedIn: linkedin.com/in/johnscafide. Legacy site NJPropertyTaxRelief.com stays live as a separate homeowner site; do not describe it as renamed or merged.

No buzzwords ("leverage," "unlock," "game-changer," "supercharge," "seamless," "empower," "next-level," "revolutionize," "cutting-edge"). No hype. No hedging filler. Short sentences. Hard rules:

- **No emojis** in any copy, including social captions and ads.
- No exclamation-point stacking. One is plenty, usually zero.

## 6. Compliance and positioning rules (non-negotiable)

- Watchdog is **not** an MLS, appraisal, title search, inspection, legal opinion, or tax-appeal conclusion. Assessments are tax-administration values, not list prices.
- **Property data is not seller intent.** Never write "seller leads," "likely to sell," "motivated seller," "distressed," "pre-foreclosure leads," "predict who will list," or anything that treats an owner as a prospect because of a public record. Approved language: "a real reason to reach out," "property-triggered conversations," "reasons to review a property," "opportunities to be useful," "know which past clients have a property reason to hear from you this week."
- Watchdog does **not** sell owner phone numbers, emails, skip tracing, or contact lists. Agents use relationships and contact data they already lawfully have. Postcards go to "Current Resident."
- No protected-class, demographic, credit, health, or family attributes in targeting or copy. No steering language about neighborhoods or who should live where. Keep ad copy about the software and the agent's work, not about housing availability, to stay clear of Meta's housing special ad category rules (if Meta flags the ad anyway, appeal with the landing page; do not add housing-opportunity language).
- Do not call any town "worst," "most unfair," "rip-off," etc. Use measurement language: "Uniformity: 62," "assessment pressure above the peer band."
- No guaranteed outcomes: no "win your appeal," "cut taxes 20%," "double your listings."
- Never fabricate purchases, reviews, screenshots of real customers, or results. Sample screens must say "sample" or "representative" and use non-identifying addresses (123 Example Street).
- Agents must include their name, brokerage, and any broker-required disclosure on anything they mail through Postcard Studio. Say so when relevant.
- Comply with TCPA and CAN-SPAM in our own outreach: no cold texting to agents without prior consent; cold email to business addresses is allowed with a working unsubscribe and a physical address; cold calls to agents' published business numbers are business-to-business but honor any do-not-call request immediately.
