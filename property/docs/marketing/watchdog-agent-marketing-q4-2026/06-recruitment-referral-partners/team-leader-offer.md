# Team leader offer

Folder: 06-recruitment-referral-partners. Owner: John runs the check-ins. Coordinator books, tracks, and writes the pilot report. Pilots start in Phase 1 and 2 and convert in Phase 2 and 3.

Who this is for: team leaders running 3 to 15 agents. One yes is 3 to 15 seats, and a team already has the thing Watchdog needs to stick: a weekly meeting where the Monday Opportunity Desk can live.

## The pilot

- 5 agents from the team, chosen by the team leader.
- 30 days.
- One 15-minute check-in a week with John, inside the team's existing meeting if possible.
- Success is defined before day one: each of the five agents records 5 property-triggered conversations in the Opportunity Desk within 30 days. That is 25 recorded outcomes for the team. A recorded outcome is the "record an outcome" action on a card (called, texted, mailed, met, no answer), not a card viewed.

Mechanics (no engineering): one invite code per team with 5 redemptions and a 30-day trial, redeemed at /beta. Pilot agents count as Founding Agents and get Founding benefits while seats remain (owner decision; recommended yes, because it is the same code type and the locked price makes the day-30 conversion easier).

Schedule: two pilots start each Monday from October 26 through November 16 (up to 8 pilots), so every pilot finishes before the Triple Play week or the week after. Target: 6 pilots started by November 20, 4 complete with reports by December 18.

## How to talk about Teams without quoting a price

Teams is controlled enrollment. Say exactly this and stop:

"Teams is controlled enrollment right now. We bring teams on after a pilot shows the workflow sticks with your agents. Pricing is set per team after the pilot, and I will not guess at it today."

If pressed: "Individual Agent seats are $59 per month or $590 per year each, and that is a fine way to start. Teams adds shared workflows and is priced separately once we know your size and what you actually use."

Never quote a Teams number, a range, a per-seat figure, or "a discount for teams." Never say "custom pricing" or "enterprise." Never promise a Teams feature that is not on the plans page. Zapier and webhooks require Pro+ or Teams; the BoldTrail (kvCORE) direct connection is in beta on Agent. Say "in beta" every time.

## What to collect on the first call

Put it on the pilot sheet before the code goes out.

1. Team size (agents, plus admins or ISAs).
2. CRM in use.
3. BoldTrail/kvCORE: yes or no. If yes, tell them the direct connection is in beta and ask if one of the five is willing to test it.
4. Farm size: how many addresses, which towns, how often they mail today and with whom.
5. Counties worked.
6. Weekly meeting day and time (the check-in goes there).
7. How the sphere is split: does each agent own their past clients, or does the team lead assign?
8. Who follows up on what today, and what "a good week" looks like in numbers they already track.
9. Who the five pilot agents are, with email addresses.
10. Whether the team leader wants to be one of the five (recommended: yes).

## What the pilot proves

1. The weekly queue creates useful conversations. 25 recorded outcomes, with the reasons behind them, is proof the team leader can read without a dashboard.
2. Agents open the desk without being told. Weekly active for each of the five, self-reported at the check-in.
3. The team leader can see it working from the check-in sheet alone. Teams reporting is not required to run a team on Watchdog today; that is a sales point, not a gap.
4. The farm and postcard workflow fits how the team already mails. If the team mails, one 50-card Postcard Studio run during the pilot at $1.79 per card is part of the plan (owner decision: whether the team's Founding 25-postcard credits can pool toward it).
5. What a Teams product would need for this team: shared client list, seat management, roll-up reporting, CRM sync. The pilot report captures this in the team leader's words.

## Conversion at day 30

Two outcomes, both fine:

- Paid Agent seats: each of the five starts an Agent plan at $59 per month or $590 per year (locked if they are Founding Agents). Agents pay individually today. [Confirm with engineering whether one card can pay for several seats; if not, say so plainly and do not promise it.]
- A Teams conversation: the team leader wants shared workflows, more than 15 seats, or roll-up reporting. John opens the Teams conversation with the pilot report in hand. Pricing is discussed there and nowhere else.

Conversion target: 3 of 5 agents to paid seats per pilot, or 1 Teams conversation opened per 2 pilots.

## RF-031: Weekly check-in agenda (15 minutes)

1. Numbers (5 minutes). Each agent: clients added, desk opened Monday (yes or no), cards acted on, outcomes recorded this week, running total toward 5. The team leader reads them out; the Coordinator writes them down.
2. One card (4 minutes). One agent walks through a card they acted on: the reason, what they said, what the homeowner said. This is the part that convinces the rest of the team.
3. What is broken (4 minutes). Confusing screens, wrong numbers, a match that failed. John writes the bug down and says when it will be looked at.
4. Next week (2 minutes). One thing each agent will do: a postcard run, a farm, a CRM upload, the BoldTrail beta test.

Check-in sheet columns: week, agent, clients_added_total, desk_opened_monday, cards_acted_on, outcomes_recorded_week, outcomes_recorded_total, best_card_reason, issue_raised, next_action

6. What was broken and what we fixed. Honest list with dates.

8. Next step. Paid Agent seats for [names] at $59 per month or $590 per year (locked for Founding Agents), or a Teams conversation on [date]. No Teams price appears in this report.
