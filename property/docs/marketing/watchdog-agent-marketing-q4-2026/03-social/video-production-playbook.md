# Video production playbook: Watchdog agent short-form, Q4 2026

Owner: John. Applies to every script in `short-form-video-scripts.md` (SV-001 to SV-065) and every row in `calendar-feed-video.csv`. The brief at `00-brief/product-and-brand-brief.md` wins any conflict with this file.

## 1. Batch filming plan

Two sessions a week. Five scripts a week. Nothing filmed the morning it posts, except SV-001 and SV-049.

| Session | When | Films | Time budget |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sunday batch | Sunday 19:00 ET | The coming Monday, Tuesday, and Wednesday scripts (3) | About 75 minutes |
| Wednesday batch | Wednesday 19:00 ET | That week's Thursday and Friday scripts (2), plus one reshoot or one bank script | About 50 minutes |

Per script, budget 12 minutes: 2 minutes to read the body aloud with a timer and cut anything over the target length, up to 3 takes on camera, 5 minutes of screen recording.

Session order, every time:
1. Screen recordings first, while the laptop is open and the sample data is loaded (Opportunity Desk, Property Lookup, Town Compare, Postcard Studio, Watchdog Score).
2. Talking heads next, same outfit, same spot, so the week looks like one series.
3. Reshoots and bank scripts last.
4. Before closing the laptop: export masters as `SV-0xx_master.mp4`, burn captions, choose the cover frame, schedule in each platform's native scheduler.

One-take rule: if you flub, pause two seconds, restart the sentence, keep rolling. Cut it in the edit. Maximum three takes. If a script will not come out in three takes, cut a sentence, not a fourth take.

Exceptions already in the calendar:
- Week 1: Sunday Sep 27 is outside the plan window, so the first session is Monday Sep 28 at 06:30 for SV-001 to SV-003. SV-001 posts the same morning. Wednesday Sep 30 films SV-004 and SV-005.
- Thanksgiving week: Wednesday Nov 25 films SV-044 to SV-046 so nothing is filmed on Nov 26 or 27. Sunday Nov 29 is a light session (SV-047, SV-048, reshoots).
- Triple Play week: Wednesday Dec 2 pre-films SV-050 to SV-053. SV-049 is filmed on location in Atlantic City the morning of Dec 7 before 07:30. Sunday Dec 6 in Atlantic City is setup and reshoots only. Wednesday Dec 9 films SV-054 to SV-056 from the hotel.
- Quiet week: Sunday Dec 20 films SV-062 to SV-065 so everything through Dec 31 is in the can before Dec 24. Wednesday Dec 23 is reshoots only. Nothing is filmed Dec 24 to 27. The Dec 27 and Dec 30 rows are optional January bank sessions.

Bank scripts (SV-018, SV-028, SV-061, SV-062, SV-063, SV-064) have no date hook. If a session is missed, post the nearest bank script and slide the dated script one slot.

## 3. Framing

- Vertical 9:16, 1080 x 1920, 30 fps. Front camera is fine.
- Phone at eye level, an arm's length away. Eyes on the upper-third line. Look at the lens, not the screen.
- Light in front of the face (a window or the LED panel), never behind. Same spot every session: clean wall or the desk with the Watchdog screen behind.
- Safe zones: keep on-screen text out of the top 12 percent and the bottom 25 percent of the frame. Platform buttons and the caption bar live there. Keep the face and the key line inside the center 4:5 area so the Instagram grid crop and the LinkedIn crop still work.
- Screen recordings: record Watchdog on the phone in portrait where the mobile view works. For desktop screens, record at 1080p, then in the editor put the screen in the top 60 percent and John in the bottom 40 percent. Zoom to the exact line being read. Never show a full page of small text.
- Every Watchdog screen carries a visible "sample" or "representative" label. Fictional addresses only (123 Example Street, 456 Example Avenue, Example Township). Never a real customer's account, name, or address.
- Car framing (Walk in knowing): parked, engine off, phone on the dash mount, side window light.
- Founder log: handheld is fine. It should look less produced than the rest.

## 4. Captions and on-screen text

- Burned-in captions on every video for every platform. Sentence case. Two lines on screen at a time, maximum. One sans-serif font, same every time [pick one and write it here]. Solid box behind the text for contrast.
- Before export, check the auto transcript for: Chapter 123, ANCHOR, PAS-1, SR1A, MOD-IV, Watchdog, ROBUST, equalization, revaluation, reassessment, Monmouth, Gloucester, Burlington, every dollar amount, every date.
- Overlays: the 3 to 5 lines from the script, each appearing when it is spoken, not all at once. The hook line is on screen at second zero.
- No emojis. No em dashes. One exclamation point at most, usually none. Sentence case, never all caps.
- Upload the SRT file to YouTube and LinkedIn as well, for accessibility.

## 5. Thumbnail rule

- The first frame is the cover: John's face mid-sentence, the hook's first on-screen line in large text across the top half. Same font and color every time.
- No arrows, no red circles, no shocked faces, no one else's face, no real address, no real screen data.
- Shorts: choose the frame manually at upload. Reels: choose the cover and confirm the 4:5 grid crop still shows the text. LinkedIn: upload a custom thumbnail from the same frame.

## 7. Turning one video into a LinkedIn text post and a carousel

Text post (use on the Tuesday or Thursday that does not carry the video, or in the same week as a second touch):
1. Line 1: the hook, exactly as spoken. Blank line.
2. Lines 2 to 7: the body, one sentence per line, trimmed to the five or six sentences that carry the fact and the "say this to the client" line.
3. Last line: the CTA sentence. Link in the first comment with the UTM.
4. Up to three hashtags at the end. No emojis. No em dashes. Sentence case.
5. Founder log and Objection scripts convert best. Tax bill translator converts well when the deadline is in the first line.

Carousel (one a week, from the video with the most saves, posted Friday 08:00 as a LinkedIn document):
1. Seven slides, 1080 x 1350, exported as PDF.
2. Slide 1: the hook, large. Slides 2 to 6: the five on-screen text lines, one per slide, each with one supporting sentence from the body. Slide 7: the CTA and the www.watchdogindex.com path.
3. Same font and color as the thumbnails. Any screenshot carries the "sample" label. No real data.
4. File name `SV-0xx_carousel.pdf`, saved in the shared assets folder [folder path].

## 8. Comment reply rules

- Reply to every comment in the first hour after each post. Set a 60-minute timer at 07:30, 12:00, 17:00, and 08:00 on LinkedIn days.
- Answer the question. Do not pitch in a reply unless the commenter asks how to get it; then use the exact offer phrase, "Start your 14-day Agent trial" or "Request a Founding Agent invite."
- TOWN comments: reply "Got it. [Town A] and [Town B] are on the list for Friday." Log them in the review sheet.
- Never confirm, deny, speculate about, or look up a specific real address or a real owner in a comment. Reply: "I don't discuss specific homes or owners in comments. Look it up at watchdogindex.com. The sources are on the page."
- "Should I appeal" or any legal or tax question: give the concept and the deadline, then "talk to a tax professional or the county board." Never a verdict.
- "Does it find sellers?": "No. Property data is not seller intent. It shows a real reason to reach out to people you already know."
- Someone calls a town a name: do not agree. Give the measurement language.
- Competitor mentions: compare the job, not the bill. Never trash them.
- Hide or delete only spam and abuse. Do not argue. Do not delete criticism.
- Reply from the account that posted, in John's voice. No emojis. No em dashes.

## 10. What not to say on camera

Drawn from section 6 of the brief. If any of these slips out in a take, reshoot the take.

- Never say Watchdog finds sellers, shows who will list, shows who is likely to move, or turns an owner into a prospect because of a public record. Property data is not seller intent. Approved wording: "a real reason to reach out," "property-triggered conversations," "reasons to review a property," "opportunities to be useful," "know which past clients have a property reason to hear from you this week."
- Never describe an owner's situation with loaded words about their finances, urgency, or hardship. Say "homeowner" and "a property reason."
- Never promise an outcome: no promised appeal result, no "cut your taxes X percent," no "double your listings," no number of calls from a postcard.
- Never call a town "worst," "unfair," "a rip-off," or "crazy." Use measurement language: "Uniformity: 62," "assessment pressure above the peer band."
- Never say Watchdog is an MLS, an appraisal, a title search, an inspection, a legal opinion, or a tax-appeal conclusion. Assessments are tax-administration values, not list prices.
- Never say Watchdog sells owner phone numbers, emails, address-to-person lookups, or contact lists. It does not. Postcards go to "Current Resident."
- Never mention a protected class or a demographic, credit, health, or family attribute. Never steer ("this neighborhood is for..."). Keep it about the software and the agent's work, not about who should live where.
- Never show a real address, a real owner, a real customer screen, or an invented testimonial. Every screen says "sample." The only approved quote is "The property story is already there when I need it," attributed to "NJ real estate agent."
- Never say the plan is free with no end date, never say a card is never needed, and never say a trial length other than 14 days. The trial sentence always includes "cancel before day 14 and you pay nothing." Never say a price other than $59 per month, $590 per year, $1,499 lifetime, $1.79 per card, or $29 for Watchdog Move.
- Never put the word "Score" after "ROBUST." The phrase is "the Watchdog Score, powered by the ROBUST Framework."
- Never shorten the product name Watchdog Intelligence. Say the full name or do not mention it.
- Never call the platform "Watchdog Index." The platform is Watchdog. Watchdog Index is a product family.
- Never describe NJPropertyTaxRelief.com as renamed, merged, or replaced. It is a separate homeowner site.
- Never promise more than 25 monitored properties on Agent.
- Never quote a real town's numbers from memory. Pull them live with the source on screen, or use a sample town.
- Never invent a statistic. The approved numbers are 564 New Jersey municipalities, 131,244 SR1A verified sales on file, 55.2 percent statewide median assessment-to-market ratio, and about 53,000 New Jersey REALTORS members.
- No buzzwords. Section 5 of the brief lists them. If a word sounds like a software ad, cut it.
- No em dashes, no emojis, no stacked exclamation points, no brochure voice.
