### IG-C03
- Date: Thu Oct 15, 2026

- Topic: Assessment is not value
- Slides (8):
  1. "The town says my house is worth $420,000." No, it does not.
  2. The assessment is a tax-administration value. It is not a list price and not an appraisal.
  3. In most NJ towns, assessments sit well below market on purpose.
  4. Statewide, the median assessment-to-market ratio is 55.2 percent.
  5. The state certifies a ratio for every one of the 564 municipalities every year.
  6. Assessment divided by ratio gives an implied market value. Useful. Not an appraisal.
  7. The line for sellers: "Your assessment is a tax number, not a price. Here is the ratio that translates it."

The conversation every NJ listing agent has at least once a month. The seller reads the assessment as the town's opinion of the price. It is not. It is a tax number, and the state publishes the ratio that explains it.

### IG-C04
- Date: Thu Oct 22, 2026
- Phase: 1
- Topic: Chapter 123 in plain words
- Slides (8):
  1. Chapter 123, explained in the time it takes to park.
  2. Every year the state certifies a ratio for each town: assessed value divided by true value.
  3. Chapter 123 draws a corridor 15 percent either side of that ratio. It is called the common level range.
  4. Take the property's own ratio: its assessment divided by what it is actually worth.
  5. Above the top of the corridor: a testable overassessment position.
  6. Inside the corridor: the assessment stands, even if it feels high.
  7. Two cautions. Revaluation years use a different standard. And a screen is not an appeal.
  8. The decision belongs to the owner and their tax attorney. The county board is the venue. Source: N.J.S.A. 54:51A-6 and the Division of Taxation common level range tables.
- Caption:

Chapter 123 sounds like a law school topic. For agents it is one idea: a corridor around the town's ratio. Above it, there is something to test. Inside it, there is not.

Knowing this keeps you from promising a client an appeal they do not have, and from missing one they do.

Watchdog shows the Chapter 123 position with the certified ratio for any NJ property and turns the range logic off in revaluation years. Free lookup in the bio.

  1. The tax surprise most buyers never see coming: the added assessment.
