# Objection handling for agents, brokers, and team leaders

Use in replies, DMs, calls, and lunch-and-learn Q&A. Each entry has the honest short answer (say it as written or close to it) and the proof or next step. Do not argue. If the objection is right for that person, say so.

---

## 1. "I already have PropStream."

Honest answer: Keep it if it earns its fee. PropStream is national and wide: lists, comps, skip tracing, $99 to $699 a month. Watchdog is New Jersey deep: assessment history, Chapter 123 position, revaluation status, added assessments, verified SR1A sales, permits, with the source attached to every number. Different job.

Proof or next step: Look up one property you know well in both. Then read our comparison page at /pricing/propstream, which carries an editorial disclosure. If the New Jersey layer is not useful, you have your answer in five minutes.

## 2. "My MLS has tax data."

Honest answer: It does. It shows the current assessment and last year's bill, pulled from the tax record. It does not show the history, the town's ratio, where the assessment sits in the Chapter 123 range, revaluation status, permits, or what changed since you last looked. Those are the things a seller or buyer actually asks about.

Proof or next step: Pull up a past client's home in the MLS, then in Watchdog. Count the questions you could answer from each.

## 3. "25 properties is not enough."

Honest answer: 25 is the monitored watchlist on the Agent plan, the properties that get change alerts. Lists and territories have their own meters, shown in the app [confirm exact list and farm capacity with John before quoting a number]. Most agents fill the 25 with past clients and live deals and rotate as deals close.

Proof or next step: Try it with your 25 most important. If you genuinely need bulk monitoring, that is Pro+ territory, and we can talk about it after the trial, not before.

## 6. "Is this a lead list?"

Honest answer: No. Watchdog does not sell names, phone numbers, emails, skip tracing, or contact lists. It watches the properties of the people you already know and gives you a property reason to reach out: an assessment change, a permit closing, a revaluation, a deadline. Property data is not seller intent, and we say that in the product.

Proof or next step: Look at one Opportunity Desk card. It shows the reason, the source date, homeowner-safe wording, and a next action. No stranger's name anywhere.

## 10. "Is this legal to use for outreach?"

Honest answer: Watchdog uses public property records and gives you reasons to contact people you already have a lawful relationship or contact basis with. It never sells owner contact information. Postcards go to Current Resident. You follow your broker's rules, TCPA and CAN-SPAM, and any do-not-contact request. I am not your lawyer and the product is not legal advice.

Proof or next step: Lesson 1 of the Academy covers the boundary. Read the data methodology page at /data-methodology and show it to your broker.

## 17. "Why not just look it up on the county site?"

Honest answer: You can, one town at a time, and you should when you need the official record. Watchdog pulls 564 municipalities into one view with history, Chapter 123 context, verified sales nearby, and permits, keeps the source attached, and emails you Monday when something material changed. The county site does not tell you what changed.

Proof or next step: Time yourself doing one past client's full picture on the county site. Then do it in Watchdog.

## 18. "What is the Watchdog Score based on?"

Honest answer: The Watchdog Score is 0 to 100, powered by the ROBUST Framework: Recourse, Overassessment Position, Burden, Uniformity, Stability, Trajectory. The evidence sits beside the score with the source and date. It is a research signal, not an appraisal, a legal opinion, or an appeal conclusion.

Proof or next step: /data-methodology explains it. Open any property and expand the evidence panel.

## 19. "Will this tell me who is going to sell?"

Honest answer: No. A public record cannot tell you what a homeowner is thinking, and any tool that claims it can is guessing. That is the point of Watchdog. It gives you a real reason to be useful to someone you already know. The agent who shows up with the right fact in the right week is the one in the room when they do decide.

Proof or next step: Read one desk card. The wording is written so you never imply the owner wants to sell.

If you start in January, you are learning the tool during the busiest six weeks. If you start now, your 25 monitored properties and your farm are already set when the notices land, and the annual plan at $590 is a 2026 expense.

Proof or next step: Start the 14-day trial now. If it is not working by day 11, cancel in one click and nothing is charged.
