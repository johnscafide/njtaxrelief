# Watchdog sales consultant program

How to structure a commission-only seller, how to present it to her, and how it is tracked inside Watchdog.

Everything money-related below is a recommendation for you to decide on. The tracking system in Watchdog reads its rates from the consultant's commission plan, so you can change any number without changing code.

## 1. The structure in one page

**Role.** Independent sales consultant. Commission only. Non-exclusive. She sets her own hours and methods, uses her own phone and car, and is paid on revenue Watchdog actually collects. She sells Watchdog plans to New Jersey real estate agents, team leaders, and brokers using the Q4 marketing kit, her own share links, and in-person conversations.

**What she sells, in order.** Lifetime first, annual second, monthly third. Lifetime is where the money is for both of you; annual is the everyday sale; monthly is the door for people who will not commit.

**Commission rates (recommended).**

| Product | Price | Rate | Her commission | Hold before payout |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Agent Founding Lifetime | $1,499 | 20% | $299.80 | 30 days |
| Pro Founding Lifetime | $3,499 | 20% | $699.80 | 30 days |
| Pro+ Founding Lifetime | $9,999 | 20% | $1,999.80 | 30 days |
| Agent annual | $590 | 20% first year, 10% each renewal | $118, then $59 a year | 60 days |
| Pro annual | $1,290 | 20% first year, 10% each renewal | $258, then $129 a year | 60 days |
| Pro+ annual | $3,990 | 20% first year, 10% each renewal | $798, then $399 a year | 60 days |
| Agent monthly | $59 | 20% of each of the first 12 payments, 10% after | $11.80 a month, then $5.90 | 14 days after each payment |
| Pro monthly | $129 | same | $25.80, then $12.90 | 14 days |
| Pro+ monthly | $399 | same | $79.80, then $39.90 | 14 days |
| Teams | negotiated | 15% of first-year contract | negotiated | 60 days |

Why 20 percent: it is what you would otherwise pay to acquire the same customer through paid media. The Q4 plan's funnel math lands around $134 per paying Agent from ads, which is 19 percent of a year of Agent revenue. Paying a person the same rate for a sale that comes with a relationship and a lower refund risk is a good trade. Why 10 percent on renewals: it keeps her caring about whether the customer stays, without paying her forever for a sale she made once.

**Accelerator (optional).** In any calendar quarter where her paid first-year revenue (lifetime plus first annual payments) passes $15,000, every sale that quarter is trued up to 25 percent. Paid as one adjustment at quarter end. This is the "big money" lever for lifetime sales without raising the base rate.

**Holds and clawbacks.** A commission is pending until the refund window closes, then eligible. If the customer is refunded inside the hold, the commission is void. If a refund happens after you paid her, the amount is netted from her next payout. No commission on refunds, chargebacks, her own account, Watchdog Move, postcards, or add-ons.

**Payout.** Monthly. By the 15th, you pay everything that was eligible on the last day of the prior month, by ACH or Zelle. The Sales Desk is her statement: date, account, base, rate, amount, status, payout reference. W-9 on file before the first payout. You issue a 1099-NEC at year end if she crosses the IRS reporting threshold for that year (confirm the current threshold with your accountant).

**Attribution.** Her share link carries her code (`?sc=SCXXXXXXXX`). Anyone who follows it and creates their first Watchdog account within 180 days is hers. For a sale she closes in person or by phone, you attribute the account to her code in the desk; any purchase from the last 45 days accrues immediately. One consultant per account, ever. You decide disputes.

**Term.** Twelve months, renewing. Either side can end it with 30 days' notice. Commissions earned before the end still pay out. Renewal commissions continue for 12 months after the end on accounts she brought, then stop.

## 2. The legal part, plainly

This is not legal advice. Have a New Jersey employment attorney read the agreement before she signs it. The specific things to ask about:

- **New Jersey's ABC test.** New Jersey presumes a worker is an employee unless the company can show all three: she is free from control, the work is outside the company's usual course of business or performed outside its places of business, and she has an independently established business. A commission-only person whose whole job is selling your product can fail the second prong. Ways to strengthen the contractor position: she works through her own LLC or sole proprietorship with its own name, sells or consults for other businesses too (non-exclusive is in the agreement for a reason), keeps her own hours and territory, uses her own tools, and is paid per result with no minimum. Do not give her a company email, an employee title, a schedule, or required daily reporting.
- **The alternative.** If in practice she will work only for Watchdog, most weeks, on your direction, the safer structure is a part-time W-2 employee paid commission plus at least minimum wage for hours worked, with the same commission table. Ask the attorney which fits.
- **Commission disputes.** Put the commission rules in writing, including holds, clawbacks, attribution, and what happens on termination. New Jersey looks hard at unpaid commissions.
- **Her outreach.** She must follow the same rules as everything else in the marketing kit: no owner-contact scraping, no cold texts without consent, business email with an unsubscribe, no seller-intent claims, no promises about features or outcomes, and she identifies herself as a Watchdog sales consultant.

## 3. What is fair for both sides

For her: high rate on the sales that matter, paid on a calendar, a statement she can see any time, a code that follows her, renewal income, Pro+ access to sell from, the whole marketing kit, and a direct line to you.

For you: cost of sale capped at what ads would cost, nothing paid until the money is collected and the refund window has closed, no fixed cost, a ledger you did not build by hand, and a person in rooms you cannot be in.

The thing to watch: lifetime sales are a one-time cash event for you and a lifetime service obligation. Pay her well on them, but keep the rate at 20 to 25 percent, not higher, because the customer's support cost continues after her commission is paid.

## 4. Earnings scenarios to show her

Assumes the recommended rates, no accelerator, and that monthly customers stay.

| Monthly activity | Month 1 commission | Month 6 run rate | 12-month total |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Conservative: 2 Agent lifetime, 4 Agent annual, 6 Agent monthly | $1,142 | $1,496 | $16,300 |
| Solid: 4 lifetime (3 Agent, 1 Pro), 8 Agent annual, 10 monthly | $2,660 | $3,250 | $36,000 |

Month 1 in the conservative row is 2 x $299.80 plus 4 x $118 plus 6 x $11.80. Monthly commissions stack as the base grows, which is why the run rate climbs. Present these as math, not promises, and say so.

## 5. How to present it

**Before the meeting.** Send her the agent trial page and the field guide so she sees the product first. Have the term sheet (section 6) printed. Have her Sales Desk provisioned so you can show it live.

**The conversation (30 minutes).**

1. What Watchdog is and who buys it, in three sentences. Agents, team leads, brokers in New Jersey. The product is the evidence beside the MLS. It sells on tax season, listing appointments, and farm mail.
2. What she would sell and why in that order. Lifetime is the big ticket and the best deal for the customer who will use it for years. Annual is the everyday sale. Monthly is the door. Show the prices.
3. The money. Walk the commission table, then the scenarios. Say out loud that the scenarios are math, not promises, and that you pay on collected revenue after the refund window.
4. How it is tracked. Open her Sales Desk. Show her code, her share links, the accounts table, the ledger with statuses, and where the payout reference lands. Say: this is your statement, you can see it any time, I see the same numbers.
5. The rules. Prices are Watchdog's prices, no side discounts. Every payment goes through Watchdog checkout, never to her. No contact scraping, no seller-intent claims. She identifies herself as a consultant.
6. The paperwork. Independent contractor agreement, W-9, the compliance rules. Attorney review on your side first.
7. The first 30 days. Agent Academy (8 lessons), a Pro walkthrough with you, her first 10 conversations with people she already knows, a weekly 20-minute check-in.

**What to say if she asks for a base or a draw.** "Not at the start. If the first 90 days show a steady pipeline I will look at a monthly draw against commission. The rates are set so that a good month pays like a good job."

**What to say if she asks for exclusivity.** "The agreement is non-exclusive both ways, and that protects you as a contractor. Your code follows your customers wherever they came from."

## 8. Agreement outline (for the attorney)

1. Parties and independent contractor status; her business entity; non-exclusive both ways.
2. Services: selling Watchdog plans; no authority to bind Watchdog, change prices, or promise features.
3. Compensation: the commission table, holds, clawbacks, attribution rules, disputes, payout schedule, statement (the Sales Desk), taxes (W-9, 1099).
4. Conduct: pricing, payments only through Watchdog checkout, marketing and compliance rules (reference the kit's compliance file), brand rules, no contact scraping, consent rules, identification as a consultant.
5. Access and data: her Watchdog account, the Sales Desk, confidentiality of customer data she sees, return of access at the end.
6. Term and termination: 12 months, 30 days' notice, payment of earned commissions, 12-month renewal tail, immediate termination for cause (misrepresentation, off-platform payments, compliance breach).
7. Intellectual property: Watchdog owns the marks, kit, and materials; she gets a license to use them while active.
8. Non-solicitation of Watchdog customers for a competing product during the term and 12 months after (ask the attorney whether this is enforceable and worth including).
9. Governing law: New Jersey.

## 10. How it works inside Watchdog

**Role.** `account_role = 'sales_consultant'` on her profile. It behaves like a normal member account with Pro+ access from a manual entitlement (`source = 'sales-consultant'`). It is not the developer bypass and has no developer tools, backoffice operator access, or admin functions.

**Her desk.** `/sales-desk` (file `property/sales-desk/index.html`). She sees her code, share links, attributed accounts (name, email, plan, status), the commission ledger, and her own prospects list, which only she can edit. The universal menu shows a Sales Desk link for her.

**Your view.** The same `/sales-desk` page, opened as the owner (developer or admin role): a list of consultants with owed and paid totals, each consultant's desk read-only plus owner tools: provision a consultant, attribute an offline sale by customer email, mark a commission paid with a payout reference, void a commission with a reason, end a consultant. The operator backoffice nav links to it.

**Attribution.** `property/js/sales-attribution.js` stores `?sc=CODE` for 180 days on the trial page, the agents page, the plans page, onboarding, and the account page, then claims it after sign-in through `claim_my_sales_attribution`. Server rules: active code, account under 30 days old, never her own account, one attribution per account. Offline sales: `assign_sales_attribution` by email, owner only.

**Ledger.** Triggers create commission rows from records that already exist: a paid Founding Lifetime purchase (`billing_lifetime_purchases`) and each paid invoice the Stripe webhook records (`billing.invoice_paid`). The base is the catalog price capped at what was paid, so sales tax never inflates it. Rates and holds come from the consultant's `commission_plan` JSON (defaults: 2000 bps lifetime, annual first year, and monthly; 1000 bps renewals; 30, 60, 14, and 30 day holds; 180-day attribution window). Refunds void held commissions and flag paid ones as clawback. Nothing moves money; you pay her and record the reference.

**Deploy.** Apply `supabase/migrations/20260929030000_sales_consultants.sql`. No Edge Function changes are needed. Confirm Stripe sends `invoice.paid` and `charge.refunded` to the webhook (both are already handled). To change rates for one consultant, update her `commission_plan` JSON in `watchdog_sales_consultants`.

**Not built.** Automatic payouts (no Stripe Connect), commissions on the Watchdog Intelligence add-on or Teams (record those with a manual adjustment row), a quarterly accelerator calculation (do the true-up as an adjustment row), and link-click counts (use the UTM campaign on her links in analytics).
