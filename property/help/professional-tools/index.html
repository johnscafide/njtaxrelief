Self-serve professional guide

Run the core Watchdog workflows without a hand-holding call.

This guide covers the three workflows most likely to need explanation: the Appeal Prospect Scanner, the Watchdog Score powered by ROBUST, and the appeal packet. Each section explains what the tool does, how to use it, and what it does not conclude.

1 · Appeal Prospect Scanner

Find properties worth reviewing, then verify before acting.

The Scanner is a municipality-level professional screening workflow. The ranking and Chapter 123 screening run server-side from governed Watchdog inputs; the browser requests and displays the entitled result rather than recreating the ranking locally.

How to run a scan

  1. Open the Appeal Prospect Scanner and choose a county and municipality.
  2. Select the sales window and minimum estimated annual savings threshold that fit the assignment.
  3. Run the scan. If a municipality does not have enough verified usable sales for the selected window, widen the window or choose another municipality rather than treating missing evidence as a zero.
  4. Sort the results by opportunity, estimated annual savings, assessment gap, sale, or evidence strength.
  5. Open a prospect to review the full record, or Quick Add it to the Watchlist for follow-up.
What the result means: a prospect is a screening candidate supported by the current governed inputs. The result is not a filing recommendation, legal conclusion, appraisal, or guarantee of a tax reduction.

Why a property can rank highly

The Scanner can use a parcel's own New Jersey-verified sale to estimate current market support, compare that support with the current assessment, and show the Chapter 123 screening position. Opportunity scoring is explainable in the interface, including its weighted parts.

Always verify before filing. Property condition, renovations, record errors, exemptions, later reassessment or revaluation activity, and stronger comparable evidence can change the conclusion.

2 · Watchdog Score

Read the score as a governed measurement, not a desirability grade.

The Watchdog Score is the 0–100 result. It is powered by the canonical ROBUST Framework v1, which explains the result through six dimensions. Watchdog tells you where the property stands; ROBUST tells you why.

R · RecoursePaths and evidence for review.
O · Overassessment PositionAssessment versus supported value.
B · BurdenTaxes relative to property value.
U · UniformityConsistency across the assessment system.
S · StabilityPressure for reassessment or structural change.
T · TrajectoryDirection of the property's tax position.

How to use it

  1. Start with the headline score to identify the current measured tax position.
  2. Open the ROBUST breakdown and inspect the six dimensions in R-O-B-U-S-T order.
  3. Use the underlying markers and source evidence to understand which facts are driving the result.
  4. Pay attention to confidence and missing evidence. Watchdog does not silently replace missing governed evidence with a guess.
  5. Use historical observations and momentum separately from the current level score when available.
Important: ROBUST is not a desirability score for a home, family, neighborhood, municipality, county, or government. It does not by itself determine an appeal, appraisal, loan, insurance decision, legal outcome, or where someone should live.

3 · Appeal packet

Turn the property evidence into a board-ready working document.

The appeal packet assembles the subject property, evidence, statutory test, and argument in the order a county board can review it. It is designed to remove repetitive research and transcription work while keeping every figure attributable.

What the packet contains

  • Subject property identifiers, property class, and current land/improvement assessment split.
  • Up to five New Jersey SR-1A usable comparable sales, ranked by timing and available physical similarity rather than by comparable assessments.
  • Municipal equalization context, including the published Director's Ratio and governed verified-sale ratio where available.
  • The Chapter 123 calculation shown line by line.
  • Municipal Uniformity context and county appeal-outcome history where available.
  • A source note for each material figure.

How to use it

  1. Open a saved property's full report and expand What happens if you file.
  2. Review the Chapter 123 position and the evidence-strength context before generating anything.
  3. Generate the packet and inspect each proposed comparable manually. Public records do not show every condition or interior-finish difference.
  4. If useful, export the larger comparable-candidate CSV for attorney or appraisal review.
  5. Use the packet as a working document alongside the current county filing instructions and required official forms.
The packet does not file an appeal. It is not a completed Form A-1 and does not pay a filing fee or serve the assessor or municipal clerk. Filing deadlines and procedures can vary by county/calendar and by whether a revaluation or reassessment was implemented, so verify the current official instructions before filing.

When something looks wrong

Fail closed instead of filling the blank with a guess.

If Watchdog says evidence is insufficient, a scan cannot run, or a Chapter 123 test is not currently supported, treat that as a data-state message rather than a product instruction to substitute an unsupported number. The safer workflow is to inspect the source record, gather independent market evidence, or wait for governed coverage.

These tools organize public-record-derived evidence and governed Watchdog calculations. Public records are not secret. Watchdog's access and anti-extraction controls are designed to add friction, entitlement enforcement, and abuse detection around the product surfaces, not to claim ownership or secrecy over the underlying public records.
