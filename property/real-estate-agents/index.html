What this guide is for

Move from “I found a record” to “I know what to ask next.”

Agents usually do not need another wall of property fields. They need a fast way to explain taxes, recognize a record change, prepare a buyer for carrying cost, spot a listing question and know when the issue belongs with a lender, appraiser, attorney, title professional or municipality.

Watchdog should shorten that path while keeping the evidence attached. Use the property record to orient yourself, then verify the source that matters to the client decision.

Boundary: Watchdog is not an MLS, appraisal, title search, inspection or legal opinion. Assessments are tax-administration values, not list-price instructions.
01 · Workflow

High-value agent workflows.

Use the same property intelligence differently depending on whether you are preparing a listing, advising a buyer or deciding who deserves a useful follow-up.

01Listing preparation

Separate assessed value from market positioning. Review taxes, sale chronology, property characteristics, permit/change history and town context before the pricing conversation.

02Buyer carrying cost

Bring current annual property tax and assessment context into the affordability discussion early, then let the lender calculate the transaction-specific payment and escrow impact.

03Sphere and farm follow-up

Use Watchlists and property-change signals to identify a real reason to reach out: an assessment change, recorded event, permit movement or other source-backed property update.

04Client-facing research

Use source-aware reports and internal Watchdog links so a seller or buyer can see what is known, what changed and what still needs professional verification.

02 · Interpretation

Read the field for the decision you are making.

A familiar number can still be the wrong number for the job. Keep assessment, tax, recorded sale, market evidence and derived signals in separate lanes.

AssessmentA tax-administration value. Read it with municipal ratio/equalization context; do not use it as a list price or appraisal.
Annual property taxA recurring carrying-cost input that can materially change a buyer's monthly picture and seller positioning.
Recorded sale historyUseful chronology and transaction context. Verify MLS facts, deed details and transaction conditions separately.
Permits / property changesA prompt to ask what changed, what closed and what documentation may still be needed.
Town / peer contextUseful for explaining whether a property's tax and assessment picture looks typical or unusual in its municipality.
Watchdog signalsA prioritization layer. Open the evidence before turning a signal into a client-facing statement.
03 · Hypothetical examples

How the data assists without using a real address.

These examples teach the workflow. They are intentionally fictional and do not imply anything about an actual homeowner or property.

Buyer affordability

Two hypothetical homes have similar asking prices, but one carries materially higher annual property taxes. The agent flags the difference before the offer and has the lender calculate the real payment impact.

Seller pricing misconception

A seller assumes the assessed value should dictate list price. The agent uses Watchdog to explain the tax context, then relies on current market evidence for pricing.

Useful follow-up

A watched property shows a new assessment or permit-related change. The agent opens the source, confirms the context and uses the change as a reason for a helpful, non-alarmist conversation.

Municipality comparison

A relocating buyer is choosing between two towns. The agent compares property-tax context while avoiding promises about a future tax bill or future assessment.

04 · Before you act

Agent decision checklist.

A strong property-intelligence workflow should expose uncertainty before it becomes a client problem.

Am I looking at an assessment, market estimate, recorded sale, appraisal or MLS fact?
Is the property-tax figure current enough for this client conversation?
Does the source cover the exact parcel and municipality?
What changed since the last time I reviewed the property?
Which part still needs lender, MLS, appraiser, title, attorney or municipal verification?
