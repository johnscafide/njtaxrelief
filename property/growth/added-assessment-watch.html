Added Assessment Watch

Surface recent Class 2 sales that look like new construction still assessed near bare-land levels. This reuses Watchdog's existing sale-to-assessment lag classification and keeps new construction separate from stale assessments and land sales.

Review candidate, not assessor prediction
What this screen means. A candidate is a recent verified sale whose assessment-to-sale ratio is materially below its municipality's current verified-sale norm and whose recorded build year falls within four years of the sale year. That is evidence consistent with new construction awaiting an added assessment, not proof that a notice will issue or a forecast of the final tax bill.

How candidates are classified

Verified-sale universe

Uses the municipality-scoped Watchdog sales endpoint backed by NJ Division of Taxation SR-1A records and only Class 2 residential sales meeting the existing price/recency screen.

New-construction classification

Matches the existing Sale-to-Assessment Lag Detector: a lagging sale is classified as new construction when a building year exists and is within four years of the sale year. Sales with no building evidence are treated as land/teardown instead.

Lag threshold

The candidate screen requires the sale's assessment-to-sale ratio to be below 80% of the municipality's governed verified-sale ratio. The displayed assessment gap is sale price × municipal ratio minus the recorded assessment.

Tax-impact follow-up

The gap is an assessment research proxy, not a tax bill. Use the Added Assessment Tax Forecaster with actual municipal tax-rate, ratio, completion-date, and comparable-value evidence before discussing dollar impact.
