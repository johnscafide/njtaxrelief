What this guide is for

Use the property record to improve the client-document question.

Clients often know the street address but not the exact property-tax amount, assessment context, recorded transaction chronology or public change history. Watchdog can orient that property file and help identify which closing statement, tax bill, invoice or other client document needs to be requested.

The platform should not blur public property data with accounting or tax-law treatment. The useful result is a better reconciliation checklist, not an automated tax conclusion.

Boundary: Watchdog does not provide tax, accounting or legal advice; establish adjusted basis, fair value, depreciation treatment or deductibility; or replace client records and current professional standards.

02 · Interpretation

What the property fields mean in client work.

Public data can help orient the file, but it should be reconciled to the documents and definitions that control the accounting or tax treatment.

Annual property taxA sourced property-cost fact that may need reconciliation to actual client payments, escrow statements and the applicable tax treatment.
AssessmentTax-administration context, not book basis, fair value, acquisition cost or a tax-return conclusion.
Recorded transactionA chronology clue that should be reconciled to closing statements, settlement records and the client's books.
Property class / use contextCan help frame questions about the asset; classification for tax and accounting purposes requires the relevant rules and evidence.
Transfer-fee resourcesUseful state transaction context. Apply the current rule to the actual closing facts rather than an old rule of thumb.
Change historyA prompt to ask whether capital work, sale activity or another property event means additional client records should be collected.
03 · Hypothetical examples

How Watchdog assists client research.

These examples are fictional and use no real residential address.

Client intake

A client provides only a street address and annual tax estimate. The accountant uses Watchdog to orient the property file, then reconciles amounts to actual tax and closing documents.

Investment-property improvement

A property shows a permit or improvement-related change. The CPA asks for invoices, completion dates and supporting records rather than inferring tax treatment from the public event.

Sale planning

A hypothetical owner asks about transfer costs. The accountant opens the current New Jersey Realty Transfer Fee resource and coordinates with closing professionals instead of relying on a stale percentage.

Assessment change

A client thinks a higher assessment automatically changes accounting basis. The CPA separates property-tax administration from the applicable accounting and tax treatment.

Client-research checklist.

What property change means I should request additional client records?
