New Jersey professional play

Follow up when permit activity changes

Use New Jersey public permit and property records to review improvement activity with useful property context.

Who this is for

Agents, contractors, lenders and property professionals monitoring New Jersey properties.

The question it answers

Has a public permit record changed enough to justify a fresh property review or client follow-up?

What public data it runs on

NJ Department of Community Affairs construction permit datasets, paired with MOD-IV property records when available.

Worked New Jersey example

Winslow Township example: A newly recorded permit for an addition can be reviewed beside the existing assessment, property class and prior record. That gives the professional a concrete reason to check the property again instead of relying on a generic annual touch.

What it does not tell you

A permit record does not by itself prove that work is complete, that a certificate was issued, or that the assessment has changed. Local construction-office records remain authoritative.
