Recourse

Paths and evidence for review. Recourse asks what the available appeal and correction context suggests when a property deserves closer investigation.

Current weight · 10%Scope · propertyCurrent model · county appeal context

What Recourse measures

Recourse is not a prediction that an owner will win an appeal. It measures the context around the ability to challenge or correct a potentially unfavorable assessment. In the current property score, the direct input is the latest county filed-appeal win-rate context available to Watchdog.

Current production mapping: the property-score implementation scales county filed-appeal win rate into a 0–100 component. Historical county outcomes are contextual evidence, not an individual probability.

Why it belongs in the score

Two properties can show similar assessment pressure and still face different practical review environments. Watchdog treats that correction context as a modest part of the overall score rather than letting it dominate the property result.

Current evidence

  • County appeal outcome history.
  • Filed-appeal reduction or win-rate context where available.
  • Property and assessment evidence used elsewhere in Watchdog to determine whether review is worth investigating.

Supporting Watchdog markers

Production also includes governed markers such as appeal evidence strength and appeal opportunity index. Those markers can deepen the Recourse experience, but they do not silently replace the current 10% score input without a versioned Watchdog Score change.

What Recourse does not mean

A high Recourse result does not guarantee an appeal win, legal remedy, tax reduction or favorable settlement. Appeals depend on admissible evidence, statutory rules, filing deadlines, the individual assessment and the facts of the case.

Research direction

Watchdog should continue testing whether county outcome context deserves to remain a top-level 10% component or whether future score versions should rely more heavily on property-specific evidence strength while keeping county outcomes as explanatory context.

Primary sources

  • New Jersey Division of Taxation · property tax appeals
  • County boards of taxation and published appeal outcome records where available.
