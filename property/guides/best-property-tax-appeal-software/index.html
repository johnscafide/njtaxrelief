New Jersey, 2026

The best appeal tool is the one that keeps research, evidence and filing rules separate.

A property database does not decide an appeal, and a filing portal does not tell you which properties deserve research. New Jersey appeal work usually requires three layers: authoritative county/state rules, parcel and comparable evidence, and a workflow that helps interpret assessment position without predicting a legal outcome.

The deadline rule matters more than software polish

Verify the responsible authority before filing.

The NJ Division of Taxation currently describes an April 1 general deadline, a May 1 deadline where a municipal revaluation or reassessment has occurred, and a January 15 alternative-calendar deadline for Burlington, Gloucester and Monmouth Counties. Software should help surface context, but the board or court remains the filing authority. A wrong deadline is worse than a missing feature.

What good appeal research software should do

  • Show assessed value and the governing ratio context
  • Distinguish evidence from prediction
  • Rank financial significance, not just percentage gaps
  • Keep sources and vintages visible
  • Make missing evidence explicit

What it should never do

  • Promise a win rate for an individual property
  • Invent a comparable or assessment value
  • Present an estimate as legal advice
  • Hide the authoritative filing source
  • Apply one statewide deadline without exceptions
