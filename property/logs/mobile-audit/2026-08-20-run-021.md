# Watchdog Mobile Audit — Run 021

- **Timestamp:** 2026-08-20 17:36 ET

- **Program:** Linear NJW-246

## Finding

The Pro buying path remained materially desktop-dense on phones even after the earlier hero/story passes. The private walkthrough form used 9px field labels, 14px inputs, an 11px submit label, 11px proof text, and 10px privacy copy. On iOS, 14px form text also creates a focus-zoom risk. The five-column Free/Agent/Pro/Pro+ comparison was still a desktop comparison grid without an explicit contained mobile overflow treatment, creating a strong risk that plan columns would either compress below readable widths or contribute to page-level horizontal overflow.

The billing cadence and plan CTA layer also needed deterministic SaaS-scale mobile targets and text sizing so a user can compare and convert without pinch-zoom.

## Typography/readability assessment

**Failed before this run.** Conversion-critical labels and actions were below the Watchdog mobile certification baseline: 9px form labels, 11px submit/proof copy, 10px privacy text, and 14px form controls. These are operational elements, not decorative captions.

After the change, form labels are 13px, fields/selects/textarea are 16px with 48px minimum height, submit text is 15px on a 52px target, form status/proof copy is 13px, privacy copy is 12px, billing controls are 14px on 48px targets, and plan CTA copy is 15px on 48px targets. Comparison cells are standardized to 13px with 12px group labels.

## Preserve vs. redesign decision

**Preserve content, redesign the mobile interaction model.** The plan comparison is important purchasing information, so it should not be hidden or collapsed into incomplete summaries. Rather than shrinking five columns into the phone viewport, the comparison is now a component-contained horizontal scroller with momentum scrolling and a sticky capability column. This keeps all plan detail available while preventing page-level overflow and preserving context as users scan plan columns.

The private walkthrough form remains a single-column mobile form, but its control sizing and typography are upgraded to normal mobile SaaS standards rather than retaining compact desktop labels.
