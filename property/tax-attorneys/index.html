What this guide is for

Use Watchdog to build the research map, not the legal conclusion.

A property-tax matter can involve assessment year, parcel identity, municipal ratio context, valuation evidence, comparable research, filing timing, county-board procedure and a record that changes over time. Watchdog can bring those research categories together around one property.

For counsel, the useful question is not “does the score say appeal?” It is “what facts do I have, where did they come from, what is missing, and what must I verify under the controlling procedure?”

Boundary: Watchdog does not provide legal advice, establish appeal merit, determine admissibility, calculate a binding valuation or replace county-board, Tax Court, assessor, appraisal or client evidence.
01 · Workflow

High-value tax-attorney workflows.

Use Watchdog as an intake and evidence-organization layer before professional legal analysis.

01Screen a potential appeal

Bring assessment, tax year, municipal context, ratio/equalization information and available market evidence into one first-pass review.

02Build an evidence checklist

Separate what Watchdog can source from what counsel still needs from the client, assessor, county board, appraiser, expert or court record.

03Track facts over time

Use case records, source dates and property-change history so a later update does not overwrite what existed when the matter was screened.

04Keep derived signals transparent

Use appeal-strength or completeness indicators to prioritize research only after opening the underlying evidence and formula context.

02 · Interpretation

What the main data should mean in an appeal workflow.

Tax-year discipline matters. A useful screen can become misleading if the assessment year, ratio year, sale period or parcel identity is wrong.

AssessmentThe challenged tax assessment. Confirm the exact tax year, property identity and official assessment notice before relying on it procedurally.
Common-level / ratio contextPotentially relevant New Jersey appeal context. Apply only the correct official ratio and controlling legal standard for the matter.
Sales / comparable contextA research starting point. Counsel and valuation professionals determine comparability, admissibility, condition and valuation relevance.
Appeal timingA critical triage field. Verify county, assessment calendar, revaluation/reassessment status and current filing requirement with the authoritative source.
Evidence completenessA Watchdog-derived view of which source categories are present or missing. It is not a legal sufficiency or success opinion.
Record changesUseful for preserving chronology and identifying facts that may have changed during a pending matter.
03 · Hypothetical examples

How Watchdog assists legal research.

Public examples are fictional and use no real residential address.

Initial intake

A hypothetical owner says the tax bill is “too high.” Counsel first separates tax amount from assessed value, confirms the parcel and tax year, and identifies what valuation evidence would actually matter.

Deadline triage

A matter arrives near a filing deadline. Watchdog surfaces a timing warning and missing evidence categories; counsel verifies the controlling deadline directly with the county board or court before acting.

Evidence gap

The public record contains assessment and sale context but no reliable support for a claimed physical condition. Counsel treats the condition as a collection task, not as a fact inferred by the platform.

Versioned record

A municipal or state source changes after the case was opened. The file distinguishes the original observation from the later record rather than silently replacing the earlier evidence.

04 · Before you act

Case-screening checklist.

Use these questions before a Watchdog observation becomes part of a legal strategy or client communication.

What exact tax year and assessment are at issue?
What is the controlling filing path and deadline for this property and county?
Which facts are source records, which are derived, and which remain client allegations?
What valuation or condition evidence is still required beyond the public-property screen?
Can the source and observation date be reproduced for every material fact?
