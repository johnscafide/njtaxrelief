Watchdog Homeowner Guide

What a revaluation or reassessment means for your New Jersey home.

Short answer: a revaluation or reassessment updates property assessments across a municipality. It can change your assessment substantially, but it does not mean every homeowner's tax bill rises by the same amount. The important question is how your property's updated value compares with the rest of the local tax base.

The purpose

Bring the assessment roll back toward current value and consistency.

New Jersey's Division of Taxation maintains a formal revaluation and reassessment program and publishes annual approval lists. A town-wide project is different from a single homeowner appealing one assessment.

The practical goal is to update how properties are valued across the municipality using a common valuation date and a consistent process.

Key point: focus on relative change. If your assessment rises more sharply than comparable properties, that may deserve a closer look. If the whole tax base moves similarly, the assessment increase alone does not tell you what the final tax bill will do.
01 · Your bill

A higher assessment does not translate dollar-for-dollar into a higher tax bill.

The local levy and total taxable value of the district determine the rate needed to raise the budgeted amount.

02 · Homeowner checklist

Review the record before you argue with the number.

Errors in the property description can affect the valuation conversation.

Confirm block, lot, qualifier and property address.
Review land and improvement values and the total assessment.
Check recorded property characteristics where available, including construction year and building description.
Look at credible nearby sales near the valuation date, not just today's listing prices.
Read the municipality's revaluation notice and current appeal instructions before relying on a general deadline.
