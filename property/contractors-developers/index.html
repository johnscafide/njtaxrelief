What this guide is for

Screen the parcel before expensive diligence begins.

Watchdog can bring basic lot, class, improvement, assessment, permit/change and mapped environmental context together early. That makes it useful for deciding what deserves a survey, municipal inquiry, engineer, architect, environmental consultant or attorney before a project assumption hardens into a budget.

The platform is strongest as a triage layer. A mapped feature or permit signal is a reason to verify, not a permit approval, zoning conclusion or regulatory boundary.

Boundary: Watchdog does not determine zoning, buildability, code compliance, permit status, wetlands boundaries, flood design requirements, engineering feasibility or regulatory approval.
01 · Workflow

High-value development workflows.

Use the early screen to decide where to spend diligence time and professional fees.

01Screen a parcel before deeper spend

Review basic parcel, class, lot, improvement and mapped-risk context before commissioning more detailed studies.

02Track permit and property changes

Use public change history to organize what appears to have changed, then verify actual permit, inspection and closeout status with the enforcing agency.

03Flag land-use questions

Use environmental and municipal context to identify questions that may require zoning, engineering, DEP or legal follow-up.

04Keep assumptions labeled

Separate source facts from development assumptions so a preliminary screen never masquerades as an approval or feasibility study.

02 · Interpretation

What the property fields mean before development.

Early data is valuable when it narrows questions, not when it is treated as a substitute for the authoritative project record.

Lot / acreage / classUseful screening facts. Verify with survey, tax map and municipal records before design or acquisition conclusions.
Existing improvementsPublic context for what is recognized on the parcel, subject to direct field and municipal verification.
Permit activityA workflow cue. Actual permit, inspection, certificate and closeout status must be checked with the responsible enforcing agency.
Assessment changeCan indicate property-record movement after work, but does not prove what caused the assessment change.
NJDEP mapped layersEnvironmental screening. Mapped features can be approximate and regulatory determinations require the proper NJDEP or professional process.
Flood contextA screening input for design and risk review, not a substitute for official mapping, engineering or applicable floodplain rules.
03 · Hypothetical examples

How Watchdog assists pre-development diligence.

These examples are fictional and do not use real residential addresses.

Acquisition screen

A developer compares two hypothetical parcels. One has a cleaner preliminary property record; the other shows mapped environmental and permit questions that justify deeper diligence before contract deadlines.

Permit closeout

A renovation appears complete, but the public change history is unclear. The contractor requests current municipal permit and inspection status instead of assuming the work is closed.

Wetlands question

A mapped wetland layer appears near the development area. The team treats it as a trigger for qualified environmental review and the appropriate NJDEP determination process.

Assessment after work

An assessment changes after construction. The owner and contractor use the timeline to frame a tax professional or assessor question without claiming the project caused the change.

04 · Before you act

Pre-development checklist.

Ask these questions before a screening observation enters a feasibility or project decision.

Which parcel facts require survey or municipal verification?
Is a mapped environmental layer approximate, advisory or regulatory?
What is the actual permit and inspection status with the enforcing agency?
Which assumptions still depend on zoning, engineering, environmental or legal review?
Did the property record change after the date of the initial diligence?
