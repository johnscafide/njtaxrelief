How to read a New Jersey property tax bill.

Short answer: your bill starts with the property's taxable assessed value and the municipality's general tax rate. The bill also identifies the parcel, tax year and installment amounts. Watchdog helps you check the public record behind those numbers before you decide whether anything needs a closer look.

Start with the identifiers

Make sure the bill is talking about the right parcel.

New Jersey property records are organized around the municipality, block, lot and sometimes a qualifier. Those identifiers matter more than a mailing address when you are matching a tax bill to an official parcel record.

Watch for: property location, owner mailing information, block, lot, qualifier, tax year, assessed value and installment amounts. If a parcel identifier does not match the property you expect, verify it with the local tax collector or assessor.
01 · The calculation

General tax rate × taxable assessed value.

The state publishes the general tax rate for each taxing district and explains that it is used to compute the property tax bill.

01Taxable assessed value

The value on the municipal tax list that is subject to the local property-tax calculation, after any applicable exemptions or adjustments.

02General tax rate

The local rate used to compute the bill. New Jersey expresses it per $100 of taxable assessed value.

03Effective tax rate

A comparison statistic published by the state. It is useful for comparing districts, but the state says not to use it to calculate an individual tax bill.

04Installments

Your annual bill is generally divided into installments. Use the due dates printed by your tax collector because local billing details control.

02 · Before you act

Three numbers deserve a second look.

A bill can be mathematically correct and still raise a separate assessment question.

AssessmentCompare the bill's assessed value with the latest municipal or MOD-IV property record.
RateCheck the correct municipality and tax year. Do not substitute an effective tax rate for the general tax rate.
Parcel IDConfirm block, lot and qualifier so you are not comparing your home with the wrong parcel.
DeductionsIf you expect a veteran, senior, disabled-person or other deduction, confirm how it appears and ask the tax collector if it is missing.
