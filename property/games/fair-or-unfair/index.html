    Sales are verified arm's-length sales from the state's SR-1A deed records. Ratios and fair ranges are the state's 2026 Chapter 123 average ratios and common level ranges. The sale price stands in for market value, so treat this as a quick check, not an appraisal. House numbers are never shown.

    How to play

    New Jersey towns don't assess homes at full market value. Each one has an average ratio, and the state sets a fair range around it: 15% either side, never above 100%. That range is called the common level range.

    • Divide the assessment by the sale price. That's how this home is assessed.
    • Above the fair range? Over-assessed. The owner pays more than their share and may have an appeal.
    • Inside it? Fair. Below it? Under-assessed, and the neighbors pick up the difference.

    Five homes a day, 20 points each. Every call is clear of the range edges, so nothing is a coin flip.

        What this teaches
