What New Jersey homeowners should know right now

Relief deadlines are moving into view, home values remain elevated, and your property record deserves a quick check before fall.

A property can look unchanged from the curb while the numbers around it keep moving. Market value, assessment data, relief eligibility, municipal changes and comparable sales all develop on different clocks. This first Watchdog Brief is about the handful of things worth checking now, before another tax notice or filing deadline makes the decision for you.

Relief update

1. Your property tax relief calendar just got important

New Jersey Treasury says ANCHOR Benefit Confirmation Letters went out in August to more than one million homeowners and renters. For many eligible residents under 65 who are not receiving qualifying disability benefits, the State plans to use prior application information to file automatically.

That does not mean everyone should ignore the paperwork. If the personal information in your confirmation letter changed, including your mailing address or direct-deposit information, Treasury says you need to submit a new application by September 15, 2026. Qualifying homeowners or renters who did not previously receive ANCHOR can submit a new ANC-1 by November 2, 2026.

Homeowners age 65 and older, along with qualifying Social Security Disability or Railroad Retirement Disability recipients, are on a different track. They must file the combined PAS-1, which is used to determine eligibility for ANCHOR, Senior Freeze and Stay NJ. The PAS-1 deadline is also November 2, 2026.

Watchdog takeawayDo not assume last year's relief status automatically tells you what will happen this year. Confirm the State has the right household, banking and property information, and know which form applies to you.
Stay NJ

2. Stay NJ is moving on two timelines

The 2024 tax-year Stay NJ benefit is still being paid during 2026. Under the fiscal year 2027 budget, eligible recipients are scheduled to receive remaining installments in August and November 2026.

At the same time, the 2025 application is setting up benefits scheduled for 2027. For qualifying senior homeowners with income up to $200,000, the State currently lists annualized maximum Stay NJ benefits of $6,500 for income up to $100,000, $5,000 for income from $100,000.01 to $150,000, and $4,000 for income from $150,000.01 to $200,000. Program availability and benefit levels remain subject to State budget appropriations.

The important distinction is simple: a payment arriving now may relate to an earlier tax year, while the application you file now can determine what happens next year.

Market pulse

3. New Jersey housing is still moving quickly

Zillow's July 31 data put the typical New Jersey home value at $584,072, up 3.6% from a year earlier. Homes were reaching pending status in roughly 19 days, and Zillow reported 26,831 homes for sale statewide at the end of July.

That does not mean every town, street or property is rising at the same pace. It does mean older assumptions about a property's market position can become stale quickly. A statewide number is context. The useful question is how your assessment, recent local sales and municipal data fit together.

Watchdog read

A fast market does not make the tax record right

Your assessed value is not the same thing as an asking price, an automated home-value estimate or a recent sale price. New Jersey property taxes are also affected by local tax rates, equalization, exemptions, municipal budgets and how your property sits relative to others in the same taxing district.

That is why Watchdog is built around the property record, not one headline number. The goal is to make changes easier to notice: assessment movement, sales evidence, municipal activity, relief opportunities and the signals that can alter the story of a property over time.

For a homeowner, the practical habit is to look before there is a problem. You do not need to become a tax assessor. You need a current baseline.

Your five-minute property check

  • Confirm the record. Check the owner, property class, assessment and core property details tied to your address.
  • Look at recent local evidence. Statewide market headlines matter less than what comparable properties are doing near you.
  • Check relief separately. Make sure you know whether ANCHOR, Senior Freeze or Stay NJ applies to your household and whether action is required.
  • Watch for change. A new assessment, revaluation, permit, sale, municipal update or relief notice can be more important than the value you saw six months ago.
