Data provenance & methodology

From public record to usable evidence.

Watchdog is not a single database and it is not a black-box estimate. A property record is assembled from identified public sources, resolved to a parcel, normalized into consistent fields, checked for usable evidence, and only then extended with calculations and screening signals. This page documents that chain.

The operating principle

A number is only useful if you can explain where it came from.

Watchdog treats provenance as part of the product. The original agency field, the parcel or geography it belongs to, the transformation applied to it, and the confidence of the resulting signal are separate concepts. A calculated value is never promoted into an “official” fact simply because it appears beside one.

1. SourceIdentify the authoritative publisher and dataset.
2. AcquireRead the published file, API or governed map service.
3. NormalizeStandardize keys, dates, values and field meaning.
4. ResolveMatch the observation to the parcel or jurisdiction.
5. ValidateCheck availability, plausibility and source status.
6. DeriveRun registered formulas only when dependencies exist.
7. ExplainSurface the result with source, scope and limitations.
Evidence classes

Source facts and Watchdog intelligence are deliberately different things.

There are four broad evidence classes in Watchdog. Keeping them distinct prevents a useful calculation from being mistaken for a government record.

Authoritative source

Published public facts

Fields such as assessment, land value, improvement value, annual tax, parcel identifier, deed references, construction year and published GIS layers are carried from the identified public source with their original meaning.

Normalized field

The same fact, made consistent

Watchdog maps source-specific names into stable product fields. For example, a source field such as NET_VALUE can be exposed as assessed value while its lineage remains attached to the original field.

Derived marker

A transparent calculation

Ratios, completeness measures, Chapter 123 screens and other signals combine governed inputs. Derived markers carry a calculation key or registered formula rather than pretending to be source fields.

Screening signal

A reason to investigate, not a determination

Flood intersections, environmental proximity, permit-record gaps and composite risk scores are triage tools. They can tell you what deserves review; they are not surveys, title opinions, code-clearance certificates, appraisals or legal conclusions.

Source registry

We track source health instead of pretending every dataset is equally ready.

Source status is operational. Live means the source is currently governed for production use in the relevant workflow. Partial means the source exists but a coverage, vintage, schema or delivery issue limits activation. Planned means an authoritative source has been identified but the mapping is not yet production-ready. Unavailable means Watchdog has not confirmed a defensible authoritative dataset for that concept.

Parcel identity & assessment

The parcel is the spine of the record.

Watchdog's property workflow is organized around the New Jersey parcel framework rather than around a person's name. The preferred stable identifier is the statewide parcel/PAMS key, with municipality, block, lot and qualifier context used to resolve and validate records. Address text is useful for finding a parcel; parcel identity is what keeps separate sources attached to the same property.

NJOGIS

Geometry is a reference, not a survey

NJOGIS describes the statewide parcel composite as an integration of county parcel data normalized across the state. The polygons support analysis and visualization, but NJOGIS explicitly says they are not survey data and do not establish legal boundaries.

MOD-IV / parcel baseline

Source fields stay traceable

Examples currently mapped in Watchdog include NET_VALUE → assessed value, LAND_VAL → land assessment, IMPRVT_VAL → improvement assessment, LAST_YR_TX → latest available annual tax, YR_CONSTR → recorded construction year, and deed/sale reference fields where published.

Resolution

PAMS PIN before fuzzy guessing

Production property resolution uses the PAMS parcel identifier where available, with municipal, block and lot keys supporting source joins. DCA permit matching likewise requires Treasury municipality code plus block and lot for property-level matching.

Tax semantics

“Annual tax” does not mean today's final bill

The public property record exposes the latest available annual-tax field from the published parcel/tax data. Municipal rates are set by tax year, so Watchdog labels older or prior-year billed amounts rather than silently presenting them as a live current-year invoice.

Important distinction: NJOGIS says the statewide normalized parcels align with Treasury's property-assessment system, but county and municipal governments remain responsible for local parcel stewardship. A newer local record can therefore exist before it reaches the statewide composite.
Sales, ratios & equalization

A recorded sale is evidence. It is not automatically a comparable sale.

New Jersey's Division of Taxation publishes SR1A sales files, equalization tables, Common Level Ranges, general/effective tax-rate tables and MOD-IV assessment files. Watchdog uses those families for recorded-sale context, assessment-to-sale relationships, municipal equalization and appeal screening. We keep the source role distinct: a deed consideration is a recorded fact; deciding whether it is a meaningful arm's-length comparable is an analytical step.

1

Read the recorded event

Use published sale/deed fields and SR1A context. Zero, nominal or structurally implausible considerations are not treated as ordinary market evidence.

2

Relate assessment to the market

Use the municipality's published ratio/equalization context to understand what an assessment implies in market-value terms.

3

Keep the tax year attached

Ratios, equalization tables and tax rates belong to specific tax years. Watchdog does not combine vintages as though every table were published on the same schedule.

conceptual equalized-value screen
implied_market_value = assessed_value ÷ municipality_ratio
example: a 55.2% ratio is used as 0.552, not 55.2

An equalized or implied market value is a derived screening value. It is not a formal appraisal and it does not incorporate interior condition, recent unrecorded renovations or every characteristic an appraiser would inspect.

Chapter 123

Appeal screening follows New Jersey's ratio framework, not a generic “taxes are high” test.

The Division of Taxation describes the Common Level Range as plus or minus 15% of the taxing district's average ratio and publishes annual Chapter 123 reference tables. Watchdog's governed Chapter 123 markers use the published common-level ratio/range together with parcel assessment and plausible sale evidence to determine whether a property appears above the upper limit.

Assessment-to-sale ratio

recorded assessment ÷ plausible recorded sale price

A sale must first be suitable for screening. The ratio is not calculated from a nominal or clearly unusable sale and called evidence.

Upper-limit comparison

assessment ratio compared with published Chapter 123 upper limit

The screen asks whether the relationship between assessment and market evidence sits beyond the permitted range, not whether the tax bill merely feels high.

Target assessment

screening-supported assessment when current ratio exceeds the upper limit

This supports an estimated reduction marker. It is not a promise that a county board will adopt the number.

Estimated annual tax difference

screening reduction carried through the property's current tax relationship

Watchdog labels this as an estimate. A successful appeal, future municipal rate and final tax bill remain outside the screen.

A Chapter 123 screen is not an appeal decision.

The State says a taxpayer must prove that an assessment does not fairly represent the applicable true-market-value or Common Level Range standard. Watchdog helps organize the public evidence and identify whether further work is warranted. Filing rules, deadlines, comparable-sale evidence and the county board's judgment still control the actual case.

Construction & development

Permit data is powerful precisely because we do not overstate what it says.

New Jersey DCA's open construction-permit dataset is reported monthly by municipalities and currently retains roughly five years of data. DCA states that most, but not all, permit-issuing municipalities report; recent records may be unreviewed; and the raw data can contain errors or omissions. Watchdog carries those limitations forward.

Property match

Treasury municipality code + block + lot

Where the DCA record provides the matching keys, Watchdog can associate permit observations with the parcel framework. We do not fabricate a street-address match when the source does not publish one.

Raw observation

Counts, dates, cost and square footage

Governed fields include permit-issued count, latest permit date, certificate count/date, applicant-reported construction cost, authorized square feet, permit type, use group, and rental/for-sale unit changes.

Derived screen

“Open permit” means record gap

Watchdog's open-permit count is a review signal based on permit-issued and certificate status records. It is explicitly not a legal finding that a municipality considers a permit open or that a code violation exists.

Municipal context

Development trends are observations, not predictions

Housing units, demolitions, construction cost and nonresidential activity are rolled into municipal context from retained DCA records. A rise in reported permits is not labeled a forecast of price, population or future development.

Normalization & joining

The difficult part is not downloading files. It is keeping unlike records attached to the right thing.

New Jersey sources are published at different geographic levels and with different identifiers. Watchdog normalizes those relationships before it derives a signal.

A

Property-level keys

PAMS PIN, Treasury municipality code, block, lot and qualifier are preferred over loose text matching. Address is a discovery aid; parcel keys provide the durable record relationship.

B

Jurisdiction-level keys

Municipal ratios, tax rates and development trends are joined at the municipality/tax-year level, not copied onto a parcel without preserving their jurisdictional scope.

C

Spatial references

Flood and environmental layers are resolved through geometry/intersection or governed proximity rules. A spatial hit is kept separate from an attribute directly published on the tax record.

When geography does not line up cleanly, Watchdog waits. The current Neighborhood Trends source is a good example: the official workbook is tract/neighborhood oriented, so a municipal marker is not activated until a governed tract-to-municipality aggregation exists.
Marker provenance contract

Every governed marker needs an answer to “show me the inputs.”

Production governance currently contains 103 provider-to-marker mappings. A mapping can identify the provider type, provider key, source keys, original source fields, calculation key, value status, freshness/cache policy and whether the marker is suitable for bulk workflows. That metadata is what separates a reusable data product from a collection of one-off calculations.

illustrative provenance record
{
  marker: "property.assessed_value",
  provider_kind: "authoritative_source",
  source_keys: ["NJ MOD-IV", "NJOGIS Parcels"],
  source_fields: ["NET_VALUE"],
  value_status: "live"
}

derived markers replace source_fields with a governed calculation/dependency chain where appropriate.
Field lineage

Original field names remain knowable

Product labels can be human-friendly without erasing the source schema. That matters when a professional wants to reconcile a Watchdog value with the underlying government file.

Value status

Unavailable is a valid answer

The property owner-name marker is currently governed as unavailable from the connected statewide public source rather than being reconstructed from another personal-data source.

Calculation key

Derived values declare themselves

Equalized-value and Chapter 123 markers point to calculation keys instead of masquerading as raw fields.

Delivery freshness

Cache life is not publication cadence

A provider's freshness/cache setting controls how Watchdog serves or rechecks a value. It does not mean the State republishes that dataset every 30 minutes, six hours or 24 hours.

Derived intelligence

Derived markers are registered formulas, not mystery scores.

The current governed formula registry contains 33 live formulas under the watchdog-derived-v3 engine family. Each entry records an operation, formula, dependencies, confidence level, status, explanation and configuration. Some are simple ratios. Others combine governed screening inputs. The formula registry is separate from the source registry because the result belongs to Watchdog, not to the publishing agency.

Assessment per acre

NET_VALUE ÷ CALC_ACRE

Normalizes recorded assessment by source acreage.

Assessment-to-sale ratio

NET_VALUE ÷ SALE_PRICE

Simple relationship between two published property fields. Suitability of the sale is a separate question.

Land share

LAND_VAL ÷ NET_VALUE × 100

Share of the recorded assessment attributable to land.

Improvement share

IMPRVT_VAL ÷ NET_VALUE × 100

Share of the recorded assessment attributable to improvements.

Tax-to-assessment rate

LAST_YR_TX ÷ NET_VALUE × 100

A Watchdog ratio of annual tax to assessment. It must not be confused with New Jersey's separately published statistical “effective tax rate,” which uses its own state methodology.

Public-record completeness

present(address, municipality, block, lot, class, assessment, tax, year built, lot area, sale date) ÷ 10 × 100

Measures presence of a defined homeowner-facing record set. Missing does not mean adverse; it means missing.

Source-authority coverage

present(12 defined authoritative parcel, assessment, tax, sale and deed-reference fields) ÷ 12 × 100

Measures whether the facts behind a professional brief are present from the governed public-record core.

Property-story confidence

40% source-authority coverage + 35% public-record completeness + 25% recording-reference completeness

Confidence in the completeness of the public facts supporting a brief. It is explicitly not a valuation or transaction conclusion.

Permit activity score

min(100, min(permit_count,20)×3 + min(open_permit_count,10)×8)

A screening priority based on governed permit observations, not a code-compliance score.

Development-constraint density

active(flood, tidal, wetlands, priority wetlands, Highlands, Pinelands) ÷ 6 × 100

Counts active governed mapped constraints. It does not determine development feasibility.

Due-diligence signal count

count(active governed flood, tidal, wetland, title-reference, environmental and jurisdictional screens)

A triage count. Every underlying signal retains its own meaning and caveat.

Transaction diligence completion

checked governed preflight sources ÷ required governed preflight sources × 100

Measures source coverage, not whether a transaction is “ready to close.”

Weighted does not mean objective truth.

Composite screens can prioritize attention by applying documented weights to governed inputs. The weight describes Watchdog's triage model; it does not transform an environmental map, permit record or title-reference screen into a professional determination by the source agency.

Confidence & missingness

We would rather return “not enough evidence” than make absence look favorable.

Confidence answers a different question from value. It describes the quality or completeness of the evidence supporting a result. Missing inputs should lower completeness, reduce confidence, neutralize a dependency or stop a calculation. They should not silently become zero, “no risk,” or “good.”

High confidence

Direct, complete governed inputs

Simple ratios and completeness measures built from present authoritative fields can carry high formula confidence because the operation and dependencies are explicit.

Medium confidence

Screening and spatial context

Permit, environmental and composite due-diligence markers are intentionally labeled as screens. Their usefulness is high, but their meaning depends on public-map coverage, matching and professional follow-through.

Partial

Some evidence exists

A provider or derived value can be marked partial when coverage, source delivery or required dependencies are incomplete. Partial is not silently promoted to live/full coverage.

Unavailable

No defensible value

If the connected public source does not expose a usable value, or an authoritative source has not been confirmed, Watchdog can leave the marker unavailable rather than filling the blank from an ungoverned source.

Freshness & source health

“Fresh” has four different meanings. We keep them separate.

1. Source vintage

The tax year, report quarter, workbook year, GIS edition or other period the publishing agency says the data represents.

2. Publisher cadence

How often the authoritative source says it publishes or processes data: annual, monthly, quarterly, rolling or irregular.

3. Watchdog source-health check

When Watchdog last checked the governed source endpoint, workbook, API or mapping relationship. A successful check does not rewrite the agency's source vintage.

4. Delivery/cache freshness

How long Watchdog may reuse a resolved provider value before rechecking it. This is an application policy, not a claim about how often government records change.

Government agencies can correct historical files, replace endpoints, revise GIS layers or publish new tax-year tables. When an authoritative source changes, the source record controls. Watchdog's calculation version tells you how a derived marker was produced; it does not freeze a government fact forever.

Current governance limitation: Watchdog has architecture for source releases, snapshots, changes and data-quality history, but this public methodology does not claim a complete historical release ledger for every source. Formula versioning and current source/provenance status are live; complete source-by-source historical replay should only be claimed where that history is actually populated.
Privacy & Daniel's Law

The product is organized around parcels, not hidden owner dossiers.

NJOGIS states that owner names have been redacted from its hosted parcels and tax-list downloads, map services and applications in response to Daniel's Law protections. Watchdog does not try to defeat that public-data choice by rebuilding the missing owner-name field from unrelated personal-data brokers. In current provider governance, owner name is explicitly marked unavailable from the connected statewide public source.

Parcel intelligence can be useful without personal-data reconstruction.

Assessment, tax, block and lot, parcel geometry, sale/deed references, municipal context and mapped due-diligence layers are about the property record. Where a user voluntarily submits a document or account-specific information, that is handled under Watchdog's privacy terms and remains conceptually separate from the statewide public parcel baseline.

What Watchdog does not claim

Useful intelligence gets stronger when its boundaries are explicit.

Not a surveyStatewide parcel polygons support mapping and analysis but do not establish legal property boundaries.
Not an appraisalPublic records do not see interior condition, renovation quality, deferred maintenance or every characteristic relevant to market value.
Not live MLS statusPublic deed and assessment data can show recorded events. They do not tell Watchdog whether a property is currently listed for sale.
Not a title opinionTidelands, deed-notice and other mapped references can trigger diligence; they do not establish insurability, ownership rights or title status.
Not a flood determinationFEMA/NJDEP intersections are screening context. The official map, lender/insurer requirements and qualified professionals control actual determinations.
Not a wetlands delineationMapped wetlands are a reference layer and can differ from site-specific delineation or jurisdictional review.
Not permit clearanceDCA's raw record feed is not a municipal certificate that a permit is open, closed, compliant or free of local issues.
Not a tax appeal guaranteeChapter 123 screening organizes public evidence. County boards, Tax Court and current filing requirements govern the actual appeal.
Not real-time government dataEach source has its own publication lag and correction cycle. A current Watchdog page can still be displaying the latest available prior-year government field.
Not “missing means safe”Unavailable or incomplete source coverage is not interpreted as proof that a risk, permit, lien, condition or constraint does not exist.
Audit a result

A professional should be able to work backward from the answer.

When a Watchdog value matters to a decision, use this sequence. It is the same discipline the product is designed around.

  1. Identify the marker. Is it a source field, normalized field, derived marker or screening signal?
  2. Identify the parcel. Confirm municipality, block, lot, qualifier/PAMS identifier and address refer to the intended property.
  3. Read the source and vintage. Determine which agency or governed public layer supplied the input and which tax year/report period it represents.
  4. Inspect the source fields. For core property data, reconcile fields such as assessment, annual tax, sale/deed references or construction year with the official record where the decision is material.
  5. For a derived value, inspect dependencies. A result cannot be stronger than the inputs feeding it. Missing dependencies should be visible as missing/partial, not assumed away.
  6. For a spatial screen, open the underlying map or agency record. The Watchdog intersection tells you where to look next.
  7. For an appeal, title, environmental, lending or appraisal decision, verify with the authority or qualified professional. Watchdog compresses the research path; it does not replace the official process.
Methodology FAQ

Questions that matter when the data is being used for real work.

Why can Watchdog and a municipal record differ?

The statewide source can lag a local assessor, county, recorder or agency update. Government publishers also correct files and layers on different schedules. When a discrepancy matters, the current authoritative agency record controls and Watchdog should be treated as a research layer, not a replacement for that record.

Does “live” mean real-time?

No. “Live” is a Watchdog integration status: the source/provider is governed for current production use. The underlying government dataset may publish annually, monthly, quarterly or irregularly. Source vintage and publisher cadence remain separate.

What happens when a source is missing?

A missing field should remain missing, reduce a completeness/confidence measure, neutralize a dependent calculation or make the result partial/unavailable. It should not silently become zero or “no issue.”

How do you distinguish an official value from a Watchdog calculation?

Authoritative fields retain source keys and source-field lineage. Derived markers carry calculation keys or formula-registry dependencies and are labeled as estimates, ratios, scores, screens or completeness measures rather than as government facts.

Why publish planned and unavailable source states?

Because identifying an official webpage is not the same as having production-grade data. A source can be blocked by an unstable export, ambiguous vintage, missing crosswalk, incomplete coverage or the absence of a defensible authoritative dataset. Publishing the state makes that limitation explicit.

Can Watchdog data be used in professional work?

Yes, as research, screening and workflow evidence, with the source and limitation kept attached. Material legal, appraisal, title, environmental, insurance, lending, tax or permitting decisions should be verified against the controlling agency record and appropriate professional process.

Found a source problem or a value that does not reconcile?

That is useful feedback. Include the property address or parcel identifier, the value in question, the source you compared it with and the date you checked it. Report a data issue or review Watchdog updates. For official tax, assessment, appeal, permit, environmental or parcel corrections, contact the agency that owns the underlying record.
