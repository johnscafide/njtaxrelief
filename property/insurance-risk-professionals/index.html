What this guide is for

Use public property context to ask a better underwriting question.

Year built, property class, public improvements, permits, environmental mapping and property-change history can make an insurance or risk file easier to orient. Watchdog brings those public signals together so a producer, underwriter or risk manager can see what may need updated evidence.

The platform is not the carrier's underwriting system. It should point to the next approved source, inspection, FEMA map, insured document or engineering review.

Boundary: Watchdog does not determine insurability, coverage, premium, replacement cost, flood-insurance requirement, loss exposure or carrier eligibility.
01 · Workflow

High-value risk workflows.

Use Watchdog to identify exceptions and route them into the carrier-approved process.

01Orient the property file

Use year built, class, improvements, assessment context and recent public changes to identify missing or inconsistent property facts.

02Screen mapped hazards

Use flood and environmental context to decide which FEMA, carrier, inspection or environmental workflow should happen next.

03Notice recent physical-record changes

Permit or property-change events can justify updated documentation, inspection or replacement-cost questions.

04Keep underwriting authority separate

Treat Watchdog as research and monitoring context, never as a carrier eligibility or coverage decision.

02 · Interpretation

What the property fields mean in risk work.

The right question is usually “what approved evidence do I need next?” rather than “what did the public record conclude?”

Year built / property classPublic-record context useful for screening, subject to carrier-approved evidence, inspection and underwriting rules.
Improvement / permit changesCan indicate that a previously reviewed risk may need refreshed documentation.
Flood mappingUse official FEMA data and current carrier rules for determinations; Watchdog is an orientation layer.
Environmental layersMay prompt further review but do not determine insurability, liability or coverage.
Assessment / taxFinancial and property context, not an insurance valuation or replacement-cost conclusion.
Source dateImportant when property condition, mapping or public characteristics may have changed over time.
03 · Hypothetical examples

How Watchdog assists risk screening.

These scenarios are fictional and use no real residential addresses.

Property record mismatch

An application describes a materially different property age or configuration than the public record. The producer flags the discrepancy for carrier-approved verification.

Flood cue

A Watchdog signal suggests flood-map review. The insurance professional opens FEMA's official source and applies the carrier's current underwriting procedure.

Recent renovation

A permit or change event appears after the last policy review. The account team asks whether updated replacement-cost or inspection information is appropriate.

Portfolio review

A risk manager monitors multiple properties for public-record changes and routes only material exceptions into insurer, engineering or loss-control workflows.

04 · Before you act

Insurance and risk checklist.

Use these questions before a Watchdog observation influences an underwriting or client workflow.

Is this source acceptable under the carrier's current underwriting rules?
What fact needs an inspection, insured document or carrier-approved source?
Am I looking at official FEMA data or a screening derivative?
Did the property materially change after the current policy was written?
Have I avoided converting public tax or assessment data into an insurance valuation?
