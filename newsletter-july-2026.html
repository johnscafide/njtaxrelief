01 • Stay NJ

Stay NJ survived, but the rules changed

There was real doubt this spring about whether Stay NJ would make it through budget season intact. The short version: it survived, the maximum benefit is still $6,500, but fewer people qualify than originally planned.

The big change is the income cap. It dropped from $500,000 down to $200,000. If you're a senior homeowner earning more than $200K, you no longer qualify for any Stay NJ benefit.

For everyone else, here's how the new tiers shake out:

Your incomeMax Stay NJ benefit
Up to $100,000$6,500
$100,000 to $150,000$5,000
$150,000 to $200,000$4,000
Over $200,000Not eligible

One more note: the $250 senior bonus that ANCHOR homeowners got last year is going away. Senior renters keep it, but senior homeowners will see that value folded into Stay NJ instead.

02 • Mark Your Calendar

Three dates that put money in your pocket

The state announced its payment schedule alongside the budget. Here's what's coming and when:

  • JULY 15 Senior Freeze payments begin. If you already applied, checks start going out on a rolling schedule based on when you filed.
  • SEPT 15 ANCHOR payments begin. Most people get paid within 90 days of this date. Many applications are being auto-filed this year, and confirmation letters go out in August.
  • NOV 2 Application deadline. This is the cutoff to file your PAS-1 for this year's benefits. If you haven't filed, do not sit on this one.

If you're a senior, the PAS-1 is one application that covers Senior Freeze, ANCHOR, and Stay NJ all at once. File it once and the state figures out which programs you qualify for.

03 • Buyers

The budget quietly helps first-time buyers too

This one didn't make many headlines, but the budget increased down payment assistance for first-time homebuyers in New Jersey.

The down payment is the wall that keeps most renters renting. Prices in South Jersey are still climbing, and saving 10 or 20 percent while paying rent feels impossible for a lot of families. Assistance programs like this exist to shrink that wall, and most people who qualify have no idea these programs exist.

If you're renting right now, or you have a kid, a niece, or a coworker who keeps saying "we'd buy if we could just get the down payment together," this is worth a real look. The NJHMFA down payment assistance program has been offering $10,000 to $15,000 to qualified first-time buyers, and this budget puts more fuel behind those efforts.

04 • Worth Knowing

Moving? Your tax benefits don't automatically move with you

Here's the thing almost nobody tells you, and it catches people every single year.

If you sell your home and buy another one in NJ, your Senior Freeze base year resets. That means the "frozen" tax amount you've been protected at goes away, and your new base starts at your new home's current taxes. For some people that's fine. For others, it wipes out years of accumulated protection.

Your ANCHOR and Stay NJ benefits are tied to your primary residence too. Sell mid-year, and the timing of your closing can affect which benefits you collect and which you leave on the table.

None of this means you shouldn't move. Downsizing can still be a massive win, especially if you're sitting on a big house with big taxes. But the order you do things in matters, and the timing matters, and that's the part most agents never bring up because honestly, most agents don't know it.
